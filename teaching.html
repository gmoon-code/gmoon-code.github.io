Secondary science teaching

I teach biology and chemistry in an English-medium international education setting in South Korea. The variation among students' English proficiency, prior science preparation, and academic experience has shaped how I design courses and how I think about evidence of scientific understanding.

Current courses

AP Biology, AP Chemistry, NGSS Life Science, and Research Methods / Capstone.

Curriculum development

I develop much of my own instructional material using AP Course and Exam Descriptions and the Next Generation Science Standards as anchors. Course materials include written explanations, diagrams, models, laboratory investigation, data interpretation, discussion, and structured practice.

I revise those materials as I learn more about the students and as classroom evidence shows where a representation, explanation, or support is insufficient.

Student research

I supervise secondary student research from question development and literature review through experimental design, data analysis, scientific writing, and presentation. I also serve as a faculty reviewer for student research proposals and completed work.

Connection to research

Classroom questions have become research questions.

Assessment, discussion, modeling, multilingual participation, and students' questions about scientific categories have repeatedly made me reconsider what a single response permits a teacher to infer and whose concerns become consequential in the scientific work underway.
