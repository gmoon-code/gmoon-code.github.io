Evidence Routes and Validity Risk in Claims About Multilingual Learners' Science Understanding in Secondary Science

A preregistered scoping review examining how secondary science research moves from multilingual learners' performances to claims about scientific understanding.

Overview

The review asks how empirical studies warrant claims about multilingual learners' science understanding when evidence appears in written responses, classroom interaction, models, drawings, and other representational forms.

The analysis focuses on the relation among evidence route, disciplinary target, learner conditions, and the judgment procedure used to move from student performance to interpretation.

Study design

Search

ERIC searched April 17, 2026.

Corpus

216 peer-reviewed journal records retrieved; 20 empirical studies met the final eligibility criteria.

Charting

Study context, science domain, evidence form, inferential pathway, disciplinary specificity, validity location, and learner-profile reporting.

Registration

Protocol preregistered on the Open Science Framework.

What the review clarified

Different evidence routes create different interpretive conditions.

Written assessment, classroom interaction, and models or drawings do not provide interchangeable access to students' scientific reasoning. Each route depends on different task, language, participation, representational, and judgment conditions.
