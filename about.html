A little about me

Hello, I'm Ye Eun. I grew up and studied in Canada and now live in South Korea, where I teach science. My background is in biology and biochemistry, though much of my day-to-day life now involves preparing lessons, talking through ideas, and making things for other people to use.

I like work that gives me the chance to return to something and make it a little clearer. A resource may look finished on my screen and still need another look once someone begins using it.

Making things carefully

A resource often begins with a small difficulty. A question feels harder to answer than it needs to, or the steps in an activity are easy to lose track of. I enjoy working out what might make that experience easier. Sometimes it means changing the sequence of a lesson; sometimes it means writing code for a tool students can use on their own.

I can spend a surprising amount of time on details that are easy to overlook, such as whether the wording of a button is clear or whether a diagram leaves room for someone to explain what they notice. I like that the things I make can change as I learn more about how people use them.

Working things through together

When I'm working with someone on a problem, I like being able to ask ordinary questions. What is this part meant to do? Could we try it another way? Have we left anything out? Those conversations sometimes lead to a small adjustment and sometimes to rethinking more than we expected.

I can become attached to a plan after spending time on it. It helps when someone points out where it isn't working, and I appreciate the chance to reconsider it together. There is usually something useful to learn from the part that didn't go as expected.

Beyond the school day

I play pungmul with an ensemble. Rehearsals can be loud and tiring in the nicest way, and I enjoy the moments when a rhythm begins to settle and we can hear how everyone fits into it. I'm glad to have people to share that time with.

I'm also very fond of stories. I like spending a long time in a fantasy world, returning to a familiar character after years away, or reading a few poems slowly and letting them stay with me. Some evenings I'm perfectly happy trying, and usually failing, to solve a mystery before the detective.
