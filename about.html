A little about me

I grew up in Canada in an immigrant family and now teach Advanced Placement Biology and Chemistry in South Korea. I studied biology and biochemistry because I was curious about the living world, and that curiosity has stayed with me. I still love following a biological question from the details of a molecule to the behaviour of an organism, then returning to see what the explanation has left out.

Family and education

As the eldest daughter in my family, I took on responsibilities early. My parents spoke little English, so I often translated and handled paperwork while learning how unfamiliar institutions worked. Money was also limited, and I worked to support my own education. Those experiences have stayed with me, especially when I think about how difficult an unfamiliar institution can be to navigate. They also remind me to ask what someone needs before deciding that I understand their circumstances.

Teaching and learning

Teaching has made me more aware of the details that I sometimes take for granted in a scientific explanation. When a student asks about a step I have passed over, I often need to return to the biology and consider it more carefully. Developing learning software brings a similar challenge. I may understand how a tool is meant to work while someone encountering it for the first time needs clearer guidance. Both experiences have encouraged me to revisit familiar explanations and designs.

Science education and identity

I grew up in a small church where my father was a pastor. Reconsidering the beliefs I inherited, alongside understanding my queer identity, was a long process. It has made me attentive to how confidently people can speak about another person's life without knowing much about it. In biology teaching, I want students to be able to examine scientific claims and categories carefully, including questions about human variation, without being asked to explain themselves personally. My research page develops the questions I am still working through.
