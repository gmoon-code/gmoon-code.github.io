A little about me

I grew up and studied in Canada and now live in South Korea, where I teach Advanced Placement Biology and Chemistry. I studied biology and biochemistry first, and I still enjoy spending time with the science itself. Teaching has given me the pleasure of returning to familiar ideas and discovering how many ways there are to explain them.

Outside my lessons, I like building things, especially small tools and activities that someone else can pick up and use. A project can begin with a single difficult question and grow as I discover what the first version leaves out.

How I approach the work

I spend a surprising amount of time on details that are easy to pass over. In a lesson, that might be an instruction that assumes students already know what a diagram is showing. In a web tool, it might be a button that makes sense to me because I built it, but gives a visitor no idea where it leads. I like finding those problems, especially when someone else sees something I missed.

Working with other people is part of what I enjoy about both teaching and making things. I appreciate being able to ask simple questions together, reconsider a plan, and acknowledge when an explanation needs more work. I have learned that giving someone room to disagree can improve the work considerably.

The science education I care about

Being a queer educator has made me attentive to the assumptions people encounter in seemingly ordinary classroom examples, as well as to what I might overlook in my own teaching. I appreciate colleagues and students who ask questions I had not anticipated. Their perspectives can require me to reconsider an explanation or a decision I thought was settled.

Life outside the classroom

I play pungmul with a queer ensemble, and I am grateful for the rehearsals, performances, and friendships that have come with it. I also love getting lost in a book, returning to favourite characters, and watching people invent stories together. On quieter evenings, a familiar mystery series is often enough.
