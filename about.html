A little about me

I grew up in Canada in an immigrant family and now teach Advanced Placement Biology and Chemistry in South Korea. My interest in living systems began well before I became a teacher and led me to study biology and biochemistry at the University of British Columbia. I still enjoy the range of the field, from molecular interactions to the ways organisms respond to their environments.

Family and education

As the eldest daughter in my family, I took on responsibilities early. My parents spoke little English, so I often translated documents and helped them navigate institutions whose rules I was learning myself. I also worked to support my education. Those experiences have stayed with me, particularly the uncertainty of trying to meet expectations that no one has explained. They influence how I think about access to education and how readily teachers and institutions assume that others know what to do.

Teaching and learning

Designing learning tools has given me another way to examine how students encounter instructions and work through unfamiliar tasks. I enjoy the small decisions involved in making something they can explore independently. In The Story Method, a hint appears before an answer, and the application distinguishes a hint-assisted recall attempt from one completed without help. Research Methods Studio lets students return to an earlier research decision as their project develops. Designing those interactions has made me more attentive to how someone else might move through an activity and where an instruction I consider clear could still leave them uncertain.

Science education and identity

I grew up in a small church where my father was a pastor. Coming to understand my queer identity while reconsidering the beliefs I inherited took time, and it taught me how limited another person's account of someone's life can be. I carry that caution into discussions of human variation in biology, where a question about a scientific category should never be taken as a request for someone to explain their identity. I want students to be able to examine the evidence behind a classification and consider its consequences while retaining control over what they share about themselves. My research page develops some of the questions I am still learning to ask.

Outside school

Outside school, I play pungmul with a queer ensemble. I also enjoy poetry, fantasy, science fiction, mysteries, and collaborative storytelling. The Interests page is a more personal collection of performances, books, and shows that I have wanted to share.
