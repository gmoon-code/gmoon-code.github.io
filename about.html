A little about me

I grew up and studied in Canada and now live in South Korea, where I teach Advanced Placement Biology and Chemistry. I studied biology and biochemistry first, and I still enjoy spending time with the science itself. Teaching has given me the pleasure of returning to familiar ideas and discovering how many ways there are to explain them.

Outside my lessons, I like building things, especially small tools and activities that someone else can pick up and use. A project can begin with a single difficult question and grow as I discover what the first version leaves out.

How I approach the work

Teaching and building software both expose the assumptions hidden in an explanation. An instruction may seem clear to its author while leaving out the relationship a student needs to see. A button may seem obvious to me because I know the program behind it. I find the work of revising those details absorbing, especially when another person notices a difficulty I had missed.

Working with other people is part of what I enjoy about both teaching and making things. I appreciate being able to ask simple questions together, reconsider a plan, and acknowledge when an explanation needs more work. I have learned that giving someone room to disagree can improve the work considerably.

The science education I care about

In a biology lesson, even a familiar illustration can imply which bodies or experiences are expected. As a queer educator, I take that possibility seriously. I also try to remain careful about what a student's question can tell me. A question about a scientific category does not give me permission to infer anything about the person asking it.
