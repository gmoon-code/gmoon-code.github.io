A little about me

I'm Ye Eun Moon, a secondary science teacher in South Korea. I studied Biology and Biochemistry at the University of British Columbia and am completing a Master of Education in Science Education. Much of what I write about here has grown from teaching students who bring different languages, experiences, and questions to the same classroom.

Where I work

I teach biology, chemistry, and life science in Incheon, South Korea. My current research and curriculum projects have grown alongside that work.

How I came to these questions

Teaching has made me more aware of how easily I can misunderstand a student's response. A missing scientific term might point to a gap in the explanation, but I may need to hear more or look at a diagram before I know what the student is trying to say. I have learned to return to those moments with more curiosity and a little more patience.

That uncertainty has shaped the research I am beginning to pursue. I am interested in how students' ideas enter classroom conversations, how scientific categories are explained and questioned, and how teachers decide which contributions to follow up. I hope to keep learning from students and colleagues as I work on these questions.

Outside the classroom

I play in a queer pungmul ensemble, where I enjoy the shared rhythm and the care it takes to perform together. I also like reading fantasy, science fiction, and poetry, and I have a soft spot for mysteries and stories people make together. Those are quieter parts of this website, and I wanted to make room for them too.
