A little about me

I grew up in Canada in an immigrant family and now teach Advanced Placement Biology and Chemistry in South Korea. I studied biology and biochemistry because I was curious about the living world, and that curiosity has stayed with me. I still love following a biological question from the details of a molecule to the behaviour of an organism, then returning to see what the explanation has left out.

Growing up and finding my way through education

As the eldest daughter in my family, I took on responsibilities early. My parents spoke little English, so I often translated and handled paperwork while learning how unfamiliar institutions worked. Money was also limited, and I worked to support my own education. Those experiences taught me how much can be expected of someone who is still trying to learn the rules of a system. They remain part of how I think about access to education, although they do not tell me what another person needs without asking.

How I approach the work

Becoming a teacher has changed how I understand knowing something. A familiar scientific explanation can become difficult again when a student asks about a step I have taken for granted. I enjoy those moments because they send me back to the biology and to the question of what an explanation actually establishes. I bring a similar habit to building educational software. An instruction or button that seems obvious to its creator may leave another person unsure what to do. Both kinds of work give me reasons to revise what I thought was clear.

The science education I care about

I grew up in a small church where my father was a pastor. Reconsidering the beliefs I inherited, alongside understanding my queer identity, was a long process. It has made me attentive to how confidently people can speak about another person's life without knowing much about it. In biology teaching, I want students to be able to examine scientific claims and categories carefully, including questions about human variation, without being asked to explain themselves personally. My research page develops the questions I am still working through.
