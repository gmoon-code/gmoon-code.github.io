Research

Research and current inquiry

My research grows from secondary science classrooms and centers increasingly on justice-oriented science education, youth participation in scientific knowledge-making, and the ways categories and representations organize what can be recognized in science.

Research interests

Justice-oriented science education; youth participation in scientific knowledge-making; power and negotiation in classroom science; decolonial and queer approaches to science education; scientific categories and representations in biology; biological sex and human variation; multilingual science learning.

Developing direction

My current projects began with questions about what teachers and researchers can responsibly infer from student responses. I am increasingly interested in what happens after a student's question or concern enters the scientific work of a classroom, especially when it exposes a limit in a category, representation, or established way of framing a problem.

Biological sex and human variation are one setting in which I have encountered these questions directly. Multilingual science learning remains another important context for thinking about what forms of expression allow teachers and researchers to see, and what they may still miss.
