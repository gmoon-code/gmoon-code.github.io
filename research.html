Research

My research concerns how teachers and researchers interpret students' scientific thinking. I am especially interested in moments when the evidence is incomplete or a student's question unsettles something the class has taken for granted.

Current and developing work

Two projects examine the interpretation of student responses. I am also beginning to explore how young people's questions about biological categories can influence the scientific work of a classroom.

Questions I hope to explore

I would like to understand what happens when students raise questions about the scientific categories and representations used in biology, particularly during lessons on biological sex and human variation. What does a teacher do with a concern that changes the direction of a discussion? How does the class decide which questions deserve further scientific attention?

I hope to study such exchanges through classroom video and conversations in which students and teachers revisit particular moments. I am also interested in designing curriculum with young people and teachers. These are developing plans, and I have much to learn about carrying them out carefully.

Students should be able to ask questions about sex, gender, or human variation without being expected to disclose anything about their own identities. I want to respect their control over what they share and take their scientific contributions seriously.
