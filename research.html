Research

A student's explanation is usually much shorter than the thinking that went into it. When I read one, I have to decide which scientific relationships are visible, what the task made possible, and what I still cannot tell.

These two inquiries let me examine that problem at different scales, through published studies of multilingual science learning and through the work students produced in my own biology classroom.

Two inquiries

Scoping review · 2026 · 20 empirical studies

When does student work support a claim about understanding?

In a review of secondary science research, I followed how authors moved from written answers, classroom conversations, and visual models to interpretations of multilingual learners' scientific understanding.

The review identifies three main routes through which student work became evidence and examines the task, language, and participation conditions that shaped those interpretations.

Classroom action research · 2026 · 28 AP Biology students

What can I learn from a corrected explanation?

I followed reflections, preparation records, and scientific explanations across three unit assessments. In a second cycle, I added a task that asked students to reconstruct an earlier mechanism after correction, without looking at their notes.

Comparing those records helped me distinguish relationships students had expressed, added, or left out, and reconsider the follow-up questions I needed to ask.
