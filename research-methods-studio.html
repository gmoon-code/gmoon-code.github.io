Research Methods Studio

A guided research workspace for secondary students who are learning to develop a question, examine evidence, plan a study, and communicate what they can reasonably conclude.

Explore Research Methods Studio
The student stays in charge

The guidance is there to help students explain their choices. Students still need to evaluate sources, decide on a suitable method, interpret their own data, and take responsibility for what they write.

Why I made it

When I supervise student research, I often notice that students can become focused on completing the next section of a paper before they have settled an earlier decision. For example, a research question may ask about an effect that the proposed measurements cannot establish. I wanted to build a place where students could work through these connections one at a time and revisit them as the study develops.

What students can work through

Literature Workspace

Students can record where they searched, screen and evaluate sources, compare studies, and connect developing claims to the evidence they have read. The workspace encourages them to notice disagreements and unanswered questions.

Methods Lab

Students work through sampling, measurements, study design, procedures, and ethical considerations. Checks before data collection encourage them to notice when the method and the research question no longer fit one another.

Data and Statistics Lab

Students can bring in a local CSV file, examine data quality, consider the variables and study design, and work toward an analysis they can explain. The goal is to keep decisions about data visible.

Writing Lab

Students can examine whether their Results, Discussion, and other sections are consistent with the study they actually conducted. Source tracing and citation checks support careful reporting.

What I am trying to make easier

I would like students to feel able to say why they chose a question, what a measurement means, and what their evidence permits them to conclude. Sometimes that involves revising a plan they were quite attached to. I hope the workspace makes such revisions feel like an ordinary and worthwhile part of doing research.
