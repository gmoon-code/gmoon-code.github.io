Research Methods Studio

This workspace helps students keep the decisions in a research project connected. Its eighteen stages move from an initial interest through the question, evidence base, design, analysis, and written account. Students can return to earlier stages when a later decision changes what the study can support.

A decision worth working through

The experimental-study guidance includes a question about assigned salinity concentrations and seedling growth. A learner could write a plausible question quickly, yet the study would still depend on decisions about treatment assignment, independent units, measurement, and the scope of the eventual conclusion.

Question

How does assigned salinity concentration affect mean seedling growth under standardized laboratory conditions?

Assignment

Decide whether the salinity treatment is assigned to individual plants or to entire trays. If a whole tray receives one condition, several plants inside it do not automatically become independent treatment replicates.

Measurement

Define growth, the observation period, and the number of independent units in each condition. Record starting size and keep relevant growing conditions consistent.

Comparison

Plan to inspect group differences alongside variation. The analysis must respect the experimental unit and the way conditions were assigned.

Claim

Explain what the comparison supports within the tested species, conditions, and time period. An observed difference alone would not establish every biological mechanism responsible.

Why the experimental unit changes the answer

Suppose each salinity condition is delivered to one tray holding several seedlings. The treatment has been assigned to trays, so the plants sharing a tray do not provide independent replications of that treatment assignment. Treating every plant as independent would make the evidence seem more extensive than the design supports. The Methods Lab asks for the experimental unit and assignment structure so students can examine that issue before collecting data.

This is a hypothetical planning example grounded in the workspace's experimental-study pathway and Methods Lab checks. It is neither a student submission nor an experiment carried out for this website.

Where the work goes

Stages 1–4

Find a direction

Explore interests and develop a question that can be investigated.

Stages 5–8

Read the evidence

Search and compare related studies, keeping track of where a claim comes from.

Stages 9–12

Plan the study

Examine design choices, sampling, measurements, procedures, and ethics.

Stages 13–15

Work with data

Consider data quality and choose analyses that fit the design and question.

Stages 16–18

Write and check

Report the study, trace claims to evidence, and examine what needs revision.

Tools alongside the stages

Literature Workspace

Record and compare sources, including agreements, differences, and questions that remain.

Methods Lab

Review the match between the research question, proposed measurements, and study design.

Data and Statistics Lab

Work with a local data file, inspect its structure, and consider what analysis is justified.

Scientific Writing Lab

Revisit how methods, results, interpretations, and citations fit together.

Saving and sharing work

The research notebook is kept locally in the student's browser. Backup and recovery options are available, and Research Chat is optional. A learner choosing to use a networked chat feature should review what project context they share and avoid entering identifiable or sensitive records.

The workspace offers questions, examples, and checks. Students still make the methodological decisions, verify their sources, and examine the limits of their own results. I designed the stages to keep those decisions visible while a project is still taking shape.
