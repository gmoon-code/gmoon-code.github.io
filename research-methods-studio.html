Research Methods Studio

This workspace guides students through the decisions involved in planning and reporting a study. The public path has eighteen stages, beginning with a research interest and continuing through reading, design, data, and writing.

Open Research Methods Studio ↗

A question to work through

One example in the workspace's research-path guidance concerns how assigned salinity concentrations might affect seedling growth. It gives us a concrete question to carry through several decisions. This is an illustration of the planning process, with no experimental results implied.

Question

How does assigned salinity concentration affect mean seedling growth under standardized laboratory conditions?

Design

Choose salinity conditions, the plant material, and a way to assign plants to groups. Decide what light, water volume, temperature, and growing time need to be kept consistent.

Measurement

Specify what counts as growth, when to measure it, and how many independent plants will contribute to each comparison.

Interpretation

Plan how to compare the groups and what limitations would remain, including the number of plants and the short observation period.

These steps illustrate questions a researcher can ask while using the workspace. They are not recorded student answers or outputs generated by the application.

Where the work goes

Stages 1–4

Find a direction

Explore interests and develop a question that can be investigated.

Stages 5–8

Read the evidence

Search and compare related studies, keeping track of where a claim comes from.

Stages 9–12

Plan the study

Examine design choices, sampling, measurements, procedures, and ethics.

Stages 13–15

Work with data

Consider data quality and choose analyses that fit the design and question.

Stages 16–18

Write and check

Report the study, trace claims to evidence, and examine what needs revision.

Tools alongside the stages

Literature Workspace

Record and compare sources, including agreements, differences, and questions that remain.

Methods Lab

Review the match between the research question, proposed measurements, and study design.

Data and Statistics Lab

Work with a local data file, inspect its structure, and consider what analysis is justified.

Scientific Writing Lab

Revisit how methods, results, interpretations, and citations fit together.

Saving and sharing work

The research notebook is kept locally in the student's browser. Backup and recovery options are available, and Research Chat is optional. A learner choosing to use a networked chat feature should review what project context they share and avoid entering identifiable or sensitive records.

Students make their own methodological choices and remain responsible for checking sources, interpreting results, and writing their work. I want the guidance to make those choices easier to examine and revise while a project is still developing.
