The Story Method

In this AP Biology learning environment, students follow named locations in a journey. A scene places scientific actors and events in a setting students can return to. Review and application activities then offer opportunities to retrieve and use the ideas again.

Walk through a journey

If you're visiting for the first time, Unit 1 is a useful place to begin. Here's a short route through the learning site.

  1. 01

    Choose a journey

    Home shows available journeys, their locations, and progress through the current unit. Choose Begin journey to enter the first location.

  2. 02

    Spend time in a scene

    Learn presents the location and its layout, introduces scientific actors, and follows what happens in the story. Optional memory anchors are available when you want to look again at a term or relationship.

  3. 03

    Try retrieval

    Quick Recall prompts appear along the route. Review offers a separate place to retrieve earlier material, with a hint available before the answer.

  4. 04

    Use an idea in a question

    Challenge Lab contains application tasks. Return to a scene or review item when a question reveals a relationship that needs another look.

What the story needs to do

The story gives each idea a place to return to. For me, the design question is whether that place helps a student recover an accurate scientific relationship. A memorable character can make a molecule easier to recall; students also need to know what that molecule does, what it interacts with, and the conditions under which the explanation applies.

That is why the environment keeps the journey, scientific actors, optional memory anchors, retrieval prompts, and application work distinguishable. I can revise the experience of moving through a scene while checking that the biology remains clear.
