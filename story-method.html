The Story Method

A story-based science learning environment that connects scientific concepts with scenes, representations, questions, and opportunities to revisit ideas across a unit. The current developed course is Advanced Placement Biology.

Explore The Story Method
What is inside

Students move from a course into units, journeys, and scenes. Scientific representations and questions sit alongside the story, with review and challenge work that allows them to revisit earlier ideas.

Why I started it

In biology, students may learn a process in one lesson and need it again much later to make sense of a different system. I wanted to make those connections easier to return to. A narrative gave me a way to organize the material over time, while keeping scientific explanations and representations close enough for students to check the details.

I began developing the project around Advanced Placement Biology. Building it has also made me think more carefully about which parts of an explanation can live inside a story and which need a diagram, calculation, or direct scientific description.

How a learning journey works

Course and unit

A student chooses a course and unit, then follows a sequence of journeys related to that content.

Scenes

Each journey is divided into smaller scenes. Scientific ideas are introduced and revisited in the course of the narrative.

Representations

Concept explanations, diagrams, graphs, data tables, equations, and other science-specific components give students a way to inspect what is happening in the story.

Review and challenge

Questions and return activities help students practice recalling and explaining relationships they have encountered before.

Working on the curriculum

The companion Content Studio is a teacher-facing workspace for organizing units, journeys, scenes, concepts, and questions. It gives me a way to revisit the curriculum as I notice gaps or need to reorganize material. Other course structures are still in development, and I am keeping the current AP Biology material as the main developed course.

The question I keep returning to is whether the story helps students make sense of the scientific relationships that matter. I am continuing to work on the clarity of the science, the links between scenes, and the opportunities students have to explain their thinking.
