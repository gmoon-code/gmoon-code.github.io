Relationships Among Post-Assessment Reflection, Reconstruction, and Later Mechanistic Reasoning in Advanced Placement Biology

A two-cycle practitioner inquiry examining how different post-assessment records relate to students' later mechanistic explanations.

Classroom problem

A corrected response could show that a student recognized what needed to change while still leaving uncertainty about whether the causal relationships underlying an earlier error had been reconstructed. The inquiry followed those relationships across later work.

Design

Setting

One Advanced Placement Biology classroom with 28 student records.

Sequence

Three successive assessments across two action-research cycles.

Linked evidence

Assessment responses, post-assessment reflections, preparation records, causal reconstructions, and later mechanistic explanations.

Analysis

Responses classified as nonmechanistic, partial mechanistic, or complete mechanistic using explicit criteria and time-delayed recoding.

What the inquiry changed for me

Records that seem related do not always converge.

The inquiry made me more cautious about treating any single reflection, reconstruction, or later response as a complete account of what a student understands.
