Interests

Pungmul

I play pungmul (풍물) in a queer ensemble (풍물패). We held a concert in 2024, have joined the Seoul Pride parade, and have busked in support of queer rights. Pungmul is a Korean communal percussion tradition with histories extending beyond the concert stage. During South Korea's democratisation struggles, pungmul groups also performed in university, labour, and farmers' movements. That history does not make every present-day performance a direct continuation of the same politics, but it gives me a way to think about why this music belongs in public gatherings as well as rehearsals. At Pride, performing together is also a public expression of belonging. South Korean queer festivals continue to take place amid disputes about LGBTQ rights and public space, making visibility a consequential part of the event. I value the relationships built through playing together, while recognising that the meaning of a performance depends on the people taking part and the circumstances around it. Books and media

Books

The Priory of the Orange Tree

Samantha Shannon · Fantasy

I love how fully imagined this world feels, from its rival traditions about dragons to the different forms of magic those stories help explain. In the East, dragons are revered; in Inys, they are feared, and those beliefs shape the worlds the characters inhabit. Within that enormous mythology, Ead and Sabran's relationship is what I find most compelling. Ead begins as a hidden protector at Sabran's court, and their growing trust and affection have to find room amid royal duty, secrecy, and danger. I love being able to follow a queer romance that belongs to the heart of an epic fantasy, with all the dragons, political intrigue, and wonder that entails. Author interview ↗

The Lord of the Rings

J. R. R. Tolkien · Fantasy

The Shire is what draws me back to Middle-earth. I love its gardens, fields, familiar paths, and the hobbits' pleasure in ordinary life. Tolkien makes those small things feel worth protecting, even as the journey takes us far beyond them. I find the homecoming especially moving. In “The Scouring of the Shire,” the hobbits return to a place that has been damaged, and caring for home means helping restore it. That makes the Shire more than a comforting beginning to the story. It is a place whose belonging depends on the people who care for it.

I'm Waiting for You

Kim Bo-young · Science fiction

I love the tenderness of the letters exchanged across impossible distances in “I’m Waiting for You” and “On My Way to You.” Two people arrange their journeys so they can return to Earth together, but delays stretch their separation across centuries. Kim lets the world change on an enormous scale while keeping the writing close to the simple wish to see someone again. Reading both sides of the correspondence makes that longing especially moving to me. I love the melancholy of it, and the imagination required to turn relativity into something so intimate. Rose

Li-Young Lee · Poetry

I love the almost physical vividness of Lee's poems. In “Persimmons,” taste, touch, and the weight of fruit carry forms of knowledge that a classroom judgment about English words misses. I grew up as the eldest daughter in an immigrant family, translating and navigating paperwork for parents who spoke little English, so that distinction stays with me. I also return to “My Sleeping Loved Ones” and “Always a Rose” for the intensity of their images and the closeness they create. My father pastored a small church, and my own relationship with faith took years to reconsider. I don't read Lee's family memories as versions of mine, but I recognise how tenderness, memory, and difficult inheritances can remain entangled. Read “Persimmons” ↗

Shows

Detective Conan

Anime · Mystery

I keep coming back for the people who make this long-running mystery feel familiar. Conan's changing relationships with Ran, the Detective Boys, and the adults around him give even an ordinary case a sense of continuity. I like that I can return to this world and recognise the characters' habits, friendships, and longstanding worries, even when I have forgotten the details of a particular mystery. There is comfort in spending time with a cast I have come to know over so many stories.

Dimension 20

Actual-play · Fantasy · Comedy

I love how the cast can take Brennan Lee Mulligan's elaborate worlds somewhere none of them expected. A ridiculous decision or running joke can gradually become part of a character's emotional story, and the players' quick responses to one another make those turns feel earned. Across settings as different as Fantasy High and The Unsleeping City, I enjoy the friendships at the table as much as the relationships unfolding in the fiction. The queer characters and stories also make these imaginative worlds especially welcoming to me. It is the combination of humour, trust, and genuine feeling that keeps me watching.
