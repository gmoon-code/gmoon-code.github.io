Interests

Pungmul

I joined a queer pungmul (풍물) ensemble because I wanted to play traditional Korean music and find community through it. I love the force of the drums and gongs, especially the energy that develops as everyone plays together. That sense of collective participation belongs to a tradition with a long history in communal gatherings. Pungmul also has a place in South Korea's 1980s democratisation movement, when university groups brought the music into labour and farmers' organizing. I am grateful to participate in one small part of a tradition with many histories. UNESCO on nongak ↗ · Research on the pungmul movement ↗

We held a concert in 2024, joined the Seoul Pride parade, and busked in support of queer rights. Playing this music as part of a visibly queer group matters to me, especially in a country where LGBTQ rights and access to public space remain contested. I value the chance to take part in Korean cultural traditions with people I feel a sense of belonging alongside, and to make that presence heard in the streets. Reporting on Seoul Pride ↗

Books and media

Books

The Priory of the Orange Tree

Samantha Shannon · Fantasy

The world of The Priory of the Orange Tree feels richly imagined to me, from its different histories of dragons to the forms of magic those beliefs help explain. Dragons are revered in the East and feared in Inys, and those contrasting traditions influence the characters' lives. Within that larger story, I am especially drawn to Ead and Sabran. Ead's role as a hidden protector places her close to Sabran while requiring her to conceal much of herself, and their growing trust and affection develop amid royal duty and danger. I love that their relationship belongs at the centre of an epic fantasy with dragons, political intrigue, and a world large enough to keep exploring. Author interview ↗

The Lord of the Rings

J. R. R. Tolkien · Fantasy

I love the Shire, with its gardens, green hills, and easy sense of home. What makes Middle-earth so absorbing is how much life Tolkien gives to the world beyond it. The hobbits travel through places with histories older than their own, meet people whose hopes and loyalties differ from theirs, and discover how far an ordinary life can be carried by events it never sought. Their journey is sustained by friendship, and mercy matters even when its consequences cannot be known. When they finally return, the Shire has troubles of its own. The landscape I love is still there, while the homecoming asks what it takes to care for a place after both it and its people have changed.

I'm Waiting for You

Kim Bo-young · Science fiction

In “I’m Waiting for You” and “On My Way to You,” two people arrange journeys that should bring them back to Earth together, only for delays to stretch their separation across centuries. I love the tenderness of their letters, which remain close to the wish to see one another even as the world changes around them. Reading both sides of the correspondence makes the longing particularly moving. Kim Bo-young uses relativity and an expansive science-fiction setting to write about love, distance, and the difficulty of waiting. Kim Bo-young on writing the story ↗

Rose

Li-Young Lee · Poetry

I return to Li-Young Lee's poems for their vividness, especially the familiar weight and texture of the things he describes. In “Persimmons,” a teacher's judgment of a word is set beside what the speaker knows through taste, touch, and family memory. Having spent much of my childhood translating English for my family, I feel particular affection for that poem. I also love “My Sleeping Loved Ones” and “Always a Rose,” where intimate images seem to hold more than the words immediately explain. I enjoy how closely Lee attends to ordinary things and how his poems stay with me after reading. Read “Persimmons” ↗

Shows

Detective Conan

Anime · Mystery

I've spent enough time with Detective Conan that its characters feel wonderfully familiar. I enjoy seeing Conan move between his life with Ran and Kogoro and his adventures with the Detective Boys. The mysteries give each episode its direction, while the friendships, worries, and little changes between people give me a reason to stay with the series. Even when I don't remember how an old case was solved, I enjoy returning to the same voices and relationships.

Dimension 20

Actual-play · Fantasy · Comedy

In Dimension 20, I enjoy watching Brennan Lee Mulligan introduce an elaborate world and then seeing what happens when the players begin making choices inside it. Someone says something absurd, everyone runs with it, and a joke can unexpectedly turn into a moment of real feeling. The cast's familiarity with one another makes that improvisation a pleasure to watch. I love the different worlds of Fantasy High and The Unsleeping City, the friendships that develop through each campaign, and the room the stories make for queer characters.
