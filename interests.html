Interests

Pungmul

I joined a queer pungmul (풍물) ensemble because I wanted to learn traditional Korean music and find people with whom I could play it. I love the force of the drums and gongs and the way a performance develops through everyone listening and responding to one another. Learning more about the music has also introduced me to histories that extend far beyond our own group. UNESCO's account of nongak (opens in a new tab) describes its place in communal gatherings, while research on the pungmul movement (opens in a new tab) examines its relationship with labour, farmers' organizing, and South Korea's democratisation movement during the 1980s. I feel grateful to participate in one small part of this living tradition.

Our ensemble gave a formal concert in 2024, and we participate in the Seoul Pride parade every year. We also give seasonal busking and smaller performances in support of queer rights. Playing together in public has given me a way to take part in Korean cultural traditions alongside people with whom I feel at home. Our presence also has a meaning beyond the music in a country where LGBTQ rights and access to public space remain contested. I am glad that we can be heard there, together.

Books and media

Books

The Priory of the Orange Tree

Samantha Shannon · Fantasy

The Priory of the Orange Tree feels as though its dragons, histories, and competing beliefs existed long before the story begins. In some parts of its world dragons are revered, while in Inys they are feared. I enjoy the way those beliefs shape the characters' choices. I am especially fond of Ead and Sabran. Ead's position as a hidden protector brings her close to Sabran even as it requires her to conceal much of herself, and their affection develops through trust as well as danger. Their relationship has a special place for me within a fantasy whose world still has so many corners to explore.

The Lord of the Rings

J. R. R. Tolkien · Fantasy

I love the Shire, with its gardens, green hills, and easy sense of home. What makes Middle-earth so absorbing is how much life Tolkien gives to the world beyond it. The hobbits travel through places with histories older than their own, meet people whose hopes and loyalties differ from theirs, and discover how far an ordinary life can be carried by events it never sought. Their journey is sustained by friendship, and mercy matters even when its consequences cannot be known. When they finally return, the Shire has troubles of its own. The landscape I love is still there, while the homecoming asks what it takes to care for a place after both it and its people have changed.

I'm Waiting for You

Kim Bo-young · Science fiction

In “I’m Waiting for You” and “On My Way to You,” two people arrange journeys that should return them to Earth together. Delays stretch their separation across centuries, yet their letters remain close to the ordinary wish to see one another. I find it moving to read both sides of the correspondence, knowing how differently time has passed for them. Relativity and the vast setting make the distance between two people feel intimate.

Rose

Li-Young Lee · Poetry

I return to Li-Young Lee's poems for their vividness, especially the familiar weight and texture of the things he describes. In “Persimmons,” a teacher's judgment of a word is set beside what the speaker knows through taste, touch, and family memory. Having spent much of my childhood translating English for my family, I feel particular affection for that poem. I also love “My Sleeping Loved Ones” and “Always a Rose,” where intimate images seem to hold more than the words immediately explain. His attention to ordinary things stays with me long after reading.

Shows

Detective Conan

Anime · Mystery

I have watched Detective Conan long enough for its characters to feel wonderfully familiar. I enjoy Conan's everyday moments with Ran and Kogoro as much as his adventures with the Detective Boys. The cases give each episode a mystery to follow, but it is the friendships, worries, and small changes in those relationships that keep bringing me back. Even when I have forgotten a solution, I look forward to spending time with those characters again.

Dimension 20

Actual-play · Fantasy · Comedy

I enjoy the moment in Dimension 20 when Brennan Lee Mulligan has introduced a carefully imagined world and the players begin finding their own way through it. A passing joke may turn into something unexpectedly tender, and the cast's ease with one another makes those changes a pleasure to watch. I love the different settings of Fantasy High and The Unsleeping City, the friendships that grow through the campaigns, and the space the stories make for queer characters.
