Interests

Pungmul

I joined a queer pungmul (풍물) ensemble because I wanted to learn traditional Korean music and find people with whom I could play it. I love the force of the drums and gongs and the way a performance develops through everyone listening and responding to one another. Learning more about the music has also introduced me to histories that extend far beyond our own group. UNESCO's account of nongak (opens in a new tab) describes its place in communal gatherings, while research on the pungmul movement (opens in a new tab) examines its relationship with labour, farmers' organizing, and South Korea's democratisation movement during the 1980s. I feel grateful to participate in one small part of this living tradition.

Our ensemble gave a formal concert in 2024, and we participate in the Seoul Pride parade every year. We also give seasonal busking and smaller performances in support of queer rights. Playing together in public has given me a way to take part in Korean cultural traditions alongside people with whom I feel at home. Our presence also has a meaning beyond the music in a country where LGBTQ rights and access to public space remain contested. I am glad that we can be heard there, together.

Books and media

Books

The Hobbit

J. R. R. Tolkien · Fantasy

The Shire is what I picture first when I think of The Hobbit. I love its green hills and countryside, and the slower pace of life that seems so closely connected to nature. Tolkien describes those places so vividly that I can imagine travelling through landscapes far beyond my everyday surroundings. The journey feels like a chance to explore a different world, and Bilbo gives me another reason to love it. I am especially fond of his kindness and of how he changes through his adventures while holding on to it.

I'm Waiting for You

Kim Bo-young · Science fiction

What moves me in I'm Waiting for You is how the desire to meet survives sudden changes in plans, difficult circumstances, and criticism from others. The characters keep waiting and searching for ways to make their reunion possible. I love that determination. Kim Bo-young's scientific imagination lets these feelings unfold across extraordinary experiences of time and distance, while the hope and longing remain easy to understand. The final story, “On My Way to You,” is my favourite. I love love, and I love reading something that cherishes it so openly.

Rose

Li-Young Lee · Poetry

I return to Li-Young Lee's poems for the rhythm of his lines and the vivid images and emotions they carry. “From Blossoms” is my favourite, especially for the movement of its words and the joy I hear in them. I also love “Persimmons.” The poem brings language, taste, and family relationships together in ways that remind me of translating English for my family when I was growing up. The sound of Lee's language stays with me as much as the images, and I enjoy returning to these poems to experience both again.

Shows

Detective Conan

Anime · Mystery

I enjoy Detective Conan for the way different characters bring their own knowledge and abilities to solving a mystery. Conan often connects clues that I would never have thought to put together, and I like discovering how he reached an answer. I also love the relationships among the characters, particularly the way Dr. Agasa looks after Conan and the children. He is my favourite, a caring, jolly scientist and inventor. His kindness is one of the reasons I find the series so comforting to return to.

Dimension 20

Actual-play · Fantasy · Comedy

One of the things I love about Dimension 20 is that I can recognise the themes of a campaign and still have no idea where the story will go. The players build it together, so an unexpected decision or exchange can change what happens next and how the characters develop. I enjoy the diversity of the characters and the different perspectives the cast brings to each situation. Watching them respond to one another is especially enjoyable when a small choice leads to a turn no one could have planned.
