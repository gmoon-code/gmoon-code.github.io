Interests

Pungmul

I play pungmul (풍물) in a queer ensemble (풍물패). We held a concert in 2024, have joined the Seoul Pride parade, and have busked in support of queer rights. Pungmul is a Korean communal percussion tradition with histories extending beyond the concert stage. During South Korea's democratisation struggles, pungmul groups also performed in university, labour, and farmers' movements. That history does not make every present-day performance a direct continuation of the same politics, but it gives me a way to think about why this music belongs in public gatherings as well as rehearsals. At Pride, performing together is also a public expression of belonging. South Korean queer festivals continue to take place amid disputes about LGBTQ rights and public space, making visibility a consequential part of the event. I value the relationships built through playing together, while recognising that the meaning of a performance depends on the people taking part and the circumstances around it. Books and media

Books

The Priory of the Orange Tree

Samantha Shannon · Fantasy

What stays with me is how the characters must learn to understand worlds and loyalties very different from their own. The scale of the fantasy is enormous, yet the relationships give that world its emotional weight. I also love that queer romance is allowed to be part of the adventure, alongside the dragons and the political upheaval.

The Lord of the Rings

J. R. R. Tolkien · Fantasy

I return to this story for the small acts of care that persist through its much larger conflict. The journey takes time, and I like that its quiet passages give companionship and loyalty room to develop. Those moments make the scale of the world feel inhabited, even when the plot is concerned with battles and kingdoms.

I'm Waiting for You

Kim Bo-young · Science fiction

I like science fiction that puts familiar emotions under unfamiliar conditions. In these stories, time and distance complicate the effort to reach someone, yet care remains recognisable. The speculative premises stayed with me partly through their sadness and partly through the tenderness of people trying to remain connected.

Rose

Li-Young Lee · Poetry

What stays with me in Rose is the way ordinary acts hold complicated relationships. In “The Gift,” a father removes a splinter while telling a story; later, the son repeats that act of care. “Persimmons” begins with a child punished for confusing English words, then gives another kind of precision to the knowledge carried through fruit, touch, painting, and family memory. In “Eating Alone,” preparing a meal brings the absent father into an otherwise ordinary day. Lee's father was a minister, and I grew up with a father who was a pastor. My own questioning of faith makes me read the collection's tenderness and authority with particular attention, without assuming our experiences are the same. Shows

Detective Conan

Anime · Mystery

I still try to solve each case before Conan does, usually without success. What brings me back, though, is the familiarity of the recurring characters and the pleasure of watching a mystery assemble itself from small clues. After so many episodes, returning to that world has become its own kind of comfort.

Dimension 20

Actual-play · Fantasy · Comedy

I enjoy watching a story take shape through decisions no single person completely controls. The cast can turn a joke or an unexpected exchange into something tender because they keep responding to one another's ideas. That mixture of improvisation, humour, and care is what makes collaborative storytelling compelling to me.
