## Classroom action research, study design, numerical classifications and case identity

The Research overview describes **one AP Biology class of 28** and a focused comparison of **10 Unit 3 student answer pairs**, with feedback-assisted correction followed by **individual closed-resource reconstruction** of the same bentazon/photosynthetic electron transport mechanism. The linked action page confirms the class size, ordinary instructional context, consent/assent and de-identification description, and the different task requirements of the three unit assessments:

- Unit 2, chytrid-related sodium transport and dehydration. **7/28** complete mechanistic explanations
- Unit 3, bentazon and electron transport/ATP/NADPH/carbon fixation. **18/28** complete on the original assessment
- Unit 4, phosphatase regulation and cancer-related cell growth. **25/28** complete

All three are **question-specific criteria**, not repeated measurements on a common scale. The two action-page tables were independently inspected as HTML text.

**Reflection table.** Of the **21** with below-complete Unit 2 answers, **12** identified a reasoning difficulty, with **10 complete / 2 below-complete** Unit 3 outcomes; the other **9** had **3 complete / 6 below-complete** outcomes. Rows correctly sum to 21 and do not establish reflection as the cause of the next assessment result.

**Original/Unit 3 reconstruction pairs.** The **ten** selected pairs include **5 extended earlier pathways**, **2 contradictions no longer repeated**, **2 earlier relationships omitted** and **1 intermediate pathway undeveloped in both**. The four patterns sum to 10. **Seven** later reconstructions met the same-item completeness criteria, but occurred after correction under a **more explicit prompt**; neither the research overview nor the action page treats this as a treatment effect or a common-ability increase.

**Cross-page S20 case.** The research overview specifically says S20's later reconstruction omitted **photosynthetic relationships previously expressed**. The action page identifies these earlier connections as involving the **proton gradient, ATP and reduced NADPH production**, omitted in the later answer. This is accurate as a written-record comparison, without claiming to know whether the student retained or lost understanding. The separate scoping case Luis is **not** a student from the action inquiry and remains different in teaching conditions and language.

**Follow-up assessment questions.** The overview expressly identifies student-specific questions as **not administered**; the action page labels its S20/S17/S06 questions retrospective and expressly states their use and effects have **not been evaluated**. These do not count as a third delivered intervention or a demonstrated student response.

**Manuscript-to-record limit.** The page is labelled **“Classroom action research · 2026 · Unpublished manuscript”** and describes records whose analysis involved permitted, de-identified original participant material after grades finalized. [C-RV22](ACTION_RESEARCH_SOURCE_AUDIT_C-RV22_2026-11-02.md) had already compared the site's claims with the author's October 2026 manuscript, supporting manuscript consistency. The confidential original classroom responses, independent rating/reconciliation files and dated consent/director authorization records **remain unavailable for independent audit**. The website's ethics wording is therefore a description of the **author-reported procedures**, not independently authenticated documents. This stage neither questions those procedures without evidence nor asserts that the original administrative records were inspected.

## Writing style, paragraph progression and necessity of change

The Research page begins with an explicitly **hypothetical biology-diagram question** to articulate inquiry into students’ ability to affect what a class investigates. The two project summaries then offer **distinct completed empirical examples** before the comparison of Luis and S20. The comparison ends with an **uninvestigated research question** about whether students could influence evidentiary standards and curriculum decisions. It never retrospectively attributes epistemic co-design to either empirical project.

The scoping page is arranged by **evidence route**, concrete ecological example, learner-profile reporting, search/eligibility methods, and scope/validity limits. The action page develops a different **chronological teacher-assessment inquiry**, with measurable task-bound classifications, response pairs, divergent student follow-ups and the practitioner’s evolving interpretation. This difference in organization prevents the two projects from sounding like interchangeable versions of a doctoral statement.

Individual paragraphs are cohesive and the transitions vary intellectually through example, methodological qualification, comparison and prospective questioning rather than through repeated overt connector words. The necessarily repeated vocabulary of **evidence, reasoning and interpretation** performs different roles in the three contexts. No current contradiction, missing qualification, or demonstrably stronger editorial replacement was found. **No public HTML edit warranted.**
