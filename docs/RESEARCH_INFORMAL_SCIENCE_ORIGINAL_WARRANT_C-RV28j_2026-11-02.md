## Burke and Navas Iannini (2021) | Original publisher abstract, no original full text verified

The original [Wiley publisher record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/tea.21714) and [ERIC indexed publication record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1310767) identify *Journal of Research in Science Teaching* **58(9), 1425–1454**, DOI `10.1002/tea.21714`, version of record first published May 2021 and print issue November 2021. The original authors used **202 student surveys** collected at **21 club sites**, with a subset of **45 children** participating in focus groups at **14 sites**, **nine staff interviews**, and fieldnotes. Participants were late-elementary to middle-school children in low-income communities in an East-Central Canadian metropolitan region, attending **out-of-school science clubs** for at least one academic year.

The publisher's abstract describes enthusiasm for practical club activity and relationships with staff. It explicitly notes that children and staff sometimes reinforced a **sharp distinction between club and school science**. Although participants described improved performance in school science, the article does not establish an independently measured achievement effect caused by attending the club. ResearchGate's identified record was labelled **publisher preview** and requested that readers obtain complete text from the authors. Additional targeted searches did not establish an independently accessible complete original article. **Do not claim that the primary article's complete methods and results were inspected.**

The website writes:

> Children attending community science clubs in Canada valued hands-on activities and the relationships they formed. Their accounts also showed how club science and school science could be treated as separate, making it difficult to connect the enjoyment of club activities with learning at school (Burke & Navas Iannini, 2021). I wonder how this distinction affects the scientific work students feel able to pursue when they move between settings.

The current summary accurately stays with participants' **reported experiences**, the club–school boundary and an explicitly personal research question. It does not make a causal claim that club attendance improved test results or imply that these club participants were the author's own students. Its paragraph closes naturally without an inflated universal or mechanical sentence. **Maintain row 11 at A (original publisher abstract/record). No public prose edit.**

## Puvirajah, Verma and Webb (2012) | Original journal PDF directly examined

The original article, “Examining the mediation of power in a collaborative community: engaging in informal science as authentic practice”, *Cultural Studies of Science Education* **7(2), 375–408**, DOI `10.1007/s11422-012-9394-2`, was read directly from the [institutionally attributed journal-formatted PDF](https://scispace.com/pdf/examining-the-mediation-of-power-in-a-collaborative-ua73qp1o6f.pdf). That PDF includes a **Western University Scholarship@Western repository cover sheet** pointing to [institutional publication item 15](https://ir.lib.uwo.ca/edupub/15), a separate author-uploaded copy cover, and the complete **Springer-published research article**. A page-image screenshot of the original **journal article's first page, PDF page 2**, was successfully inspected, verifying title, author names, journal volume/pages/year and DOI. The [first-party Springer journal record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11422-012-9394-2) and [original author faculty listing](https://www.edu.uwo.ca/about/faculty-profiles/anton-puvirajah/index.html) corroborate bibliographic identity.

The original article's methods, participant description, data sources, coding/analysis procedures, selected discourse excerpts, interpretation and explicit researcher-positionality limitations were consulted. The focal study participants were **seven high-school students and two mentors** drawn from a larger club of **15 students and five mentors**. They were from two high schools in a suburban southeastern-US metropolitan area. The selected setting was a **robotics competition**, with video/audio observations of the competition, transcripts, student and mentor interviews conducted shortly after competition, researcher notes and artefacts. The team used **critical discourse analysis**, including Gee's Building Tasks, to interpret the ways power, identities, relationships and epistemic engagement were negotiated through interaction.

The source interprets these interactions as supporting some **symmetry of authority and co-construction of knowledge claims** in an informal collaborative setting. It explicitly recognises its situated, interpretive analytic method and the researcher's own involvement/position. These data are **not a randomized trial** and do not establish that any particular activity will produce equal authority in ordinary school science lessons.

The website states:

> Relationships and learning settings shape the authority students can exercise. A longitudinal study followed an early-career teacher negotiating an activist commitment alongside the demands of school practice (Barrett, 2013). In a different setting, students and mentors participating in a robotics competition negotiated authority as they developed scientific and technical knowledge together (Puvirajah et al., 2012). Productive disciplinary engagement provides a further account of how student authority can develop alongside responsibility to peers and access to disciplinary resources (Engle & Conant, 2002).

This accurately distinguishes the **different research settings** and describes the **robotics competition** without inflating the seven selected participants into all students of the school. The final sentence draws on a separately attributed conceptual framework, rather than treating the informal project as an evaluation of a school intervention. The paragraph's transitions carry distinct conceptual functions and already meet the author's precision, gentle voice and varied prose preferences. **No public edit warranted.**
