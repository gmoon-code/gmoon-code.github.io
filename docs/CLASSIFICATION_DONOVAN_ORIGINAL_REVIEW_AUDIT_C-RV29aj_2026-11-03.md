## Specific evidence from the original review

1. The review differentiates psychological/genetic essentialism from biological knowledge itself. It discusses how teaching monogenic conditions as group-specific traits can cue inferences of racial uniformity and discreteness beyond the condition actually measured; this is a claim about **reasoning triggered by particular instructional representations**.
2. In the section on basic genetics instruction, Donovan reports **three randomized controlled trials** involving **middle/secondary-school students**. For an eighth-grade comparison (**n = 43**), students receiving genetics materials that categorized sickle-cell disease prevalence by race were compared with students learning about the prevalence without race. The former group more often endorsed genetic differences between racial groups in intelligence, science ability and academic ability. The review reports two later replicated comparisons among grades **7–9**. These descriptions directly support the essay's **“Randomized studies reviewed by Donovan (2022)”** and **“than among students in comparison conditions.”**
3. This is a **review of studies**, not an experimental result generated by Donovan's 2022 review itself. The essay correctly attributes the experiments as **reviewed by** Donovan. Do not elevate the 2022 work to a new clinical or classroom trial, and do not imply all instructional uses of racial terminology have the same effect.
4. The review also summarizes interventions that teach patterns of variation and test anti-essentialist interpretations, including a crossover experiment, additional randomized comparisons and a quasi-experimental six-week unit. These point to **instructional context and purpose** as relevant, not a claim that any use of group labels produces essentialism. The essay makes the narrower inferential point about racial labels being treated as explanations of genotype.
5. Important scope limitations remain. Most experiments discussed concern particular U.S. school contexts, and the review acknowledges unresolved questions about transfer beyond class, variation across students, persistence of effects and general education practice. The review's claims about broad consequences of ordinary genetics education are explicitly framed in part as hypotheses. **The essay does not report a direct measured change in the author's Korean secondary school classes, a new locally tested intervention, or durable population-wide attitudinal effects.**

Original review body passages checked particularly in the subsection **“The influence of basic genetics instruction on belief in genetic essentialism”**, and adjacent **“The influence of standard genomics instruction”**, **“The influence of humane genomics instruction”**, and **“Implications for genetics education research”**. Reference identity independently corroborated by the publisher record and PubMed article metadata.

## Claim, neighbouring sentences and editorial determination

The essay's fourth HbS paragraph moves from the **mismatch between racial identity and individual HbS genotype** to the more general epistemic consequence of representing genetically linked disease as a property of racial groups. The Donovan sentence then provides secondary-school intervention evidence, and the following sentence makes the restrained inference that category choice **can shape** what students infer beyond one condition. This progression does not claim that the Kenyan Williams cohorts assessed race-based instruction, that haemoglobin S disease prevalence directly demonstrates beliefs in racial essence, or that the essay's proposed lessons have been implemented.

The preceding three paragraphs distinguish **HbAS genotype**, **clinical malaria outcomes in Kenyan child cohorts**, and **population-genetic/reproductive fitness models**; the observed 50%, 75% and almost 90% protection values are not converted to genotype fitness coefficients. The succeeding sex-chromosome section separately discusses historical XYY behavioural attributions and an insufficient-evidence review, without assimilating XYY to racial genetic-disease instruction. The curriculum and student-authority sections keep their future examples explicitly hypothetical and disallow personal disclosure about identity, ancestry or medical information.

Editorially, the focal sentence is specific enough to communicate the review's experimental finding while naming Donovan's role as a reviewer. Substituting generic “framing can affect beliefs” would remove valid specificity; inserting a taxonomy or an expanded definition of genetic essentialism into the already developed paragraph would increase density and interrupt its progression. The following sentence gives a measured interpretation in the author's gentle voice. Its use of **“can”** rather than guaranteed causal generalization is justified. No em dash, prose colon, corrective contrast, unsupported affective conclusion, or mechanically repeated transition requires correction.

**Decision:** **No public prose or bibliography changes.** This is a **source-provenance and verification upgrade** confined to the essay's Donovan reference. It **does not change** `research.html` or the **42 F / 10 A / 1 M / 0 P** inventory of that *different* page's 53 references.
