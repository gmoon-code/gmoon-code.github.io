## Exact public statement and conceptual comparison

The current page writes:

> Questions about authority also concern whose contributions are recognized as legitimate. Epistemic agency involves more than performing prescribed scientific practices (Miller et al., 2018).

Miller and colleagues distinguish students' participation in science practices selected by teachers/reform designers from students' opportunities to **shape and evaluate knowledge and the rules of knowledge-building**. They define epistemic agency through students being positioned with, perceiving and acting on opportunities to shape classroom community knowledge work. Their conceptual examples include the use of student knowledge, construction and public value of a knowledge product, and the possibility of changing structures that constrain action. Importantly, these are **analytic opportunities**, not four mutually exclusive experimentally validated components.

The article's opening seventh-grade invasive-spiny-water-flea classroom account is a **vignette used in a conceptual argument**, rather than evidence of a particular measured causal intervention. The website does not describe it as a directly measured empirical effect or equate agency with students merely being assigned scientific tasks. The later page question about whether students can challenge the grounds on which a teacher corrects an explanation is presented as the author's future inquiry, not an outcome proved by Miller et al.
