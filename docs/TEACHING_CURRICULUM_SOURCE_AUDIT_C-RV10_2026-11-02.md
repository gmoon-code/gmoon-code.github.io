## Independent numerical verification

**AP Chemistry illustrative system**

`N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃` and `Qp = P(NH₃)²/[P(N₂)P(H₂)³]`.

- Initial hypothetical pressures `1.0, 2.0, 1.0 atm` give `Qp=1/(1×8)=0.125`, below `Kp=0.50`; net tendency toward NH₃.
- The *separate* mixture at equilibrium `1.0, 2.0, 2.0 atm` gives `Qp=4/8=0.50`. Halving volume immediately doubles all partial pressures, giving `Qp,new=4²/(2×4³)=0.125`, one quarter of Kp. Subsequent re-equilibration is distinguished from immediate pressure change. Kp stays fixed at the stated temperature.
- Doubling the volume instead halves these pressures and gives `Qp=1²/(0.5×1³)=2.0`, so the net direction reverses. The catalyst answer correctly distinguishes reaction speed from equilibrium composition.

All calculations are explicitly constructed problems rather than industrial measurements or observed student errors. The page also notes that rigorous activities are dimensionless under standard-state conventions.

**AP Biology evolution model**

Initial `p=0.8,q=0.2` implies Hardy–Weinberg proportions `p²=0.64, 2pq=0.32, q²=0.04`, with `0.96` carrying at least one HbA allele under that initial hypothetical assumption. Then `q′=(0.320/2+0.008)/0.776=0.216494845...`, yielding next-generation zygote proportions approximately **0.6139, 0.3392, 0.0469** under random mating. Every reported value is mathematically accurate within its displayed rounding.
