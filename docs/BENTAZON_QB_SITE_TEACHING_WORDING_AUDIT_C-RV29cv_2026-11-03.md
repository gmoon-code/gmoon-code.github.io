## Scientific distinction and significance for the study

The ordinary site-of-action account is that PSII-inhibiting herbicides interfere with transfer toward the secondary plastoquinone acceptor **Q_B**, competing with native plastoquinone at a binding pocket of the **D1 protein**. A phrase saying the herbicide binds competitively **to plastoquinone itself** instead identifies the displaced endogenous molecule as the binding partner. The edited classroom form's wording is therefore scientifically imprecise and could affect which intermediate process a student thinks is being blocked. The exact history and use of that private form are unknown.

The main 2021 plant study further indicates substantial **differences in specificity and inhibition strength among herbicides**. Its Bentazon results had relatively weak inhibitory effect and a less specific D1 Q_B interaction compared with diuron, metribuzin and terbuthylazine, under the studied pea thylakoid assay and docking conditions. That observation is not evidence that Bentazon never inhibits PSII, nor should a classroom task premise about inhibition be promoted to an unconditional claim about dose, injury or whole-plant death.

These source distinctions matter for interpreting the **relative prompt scaffolding** in a study of mechanistic reasoning. The longer edited form provides information about the electron carrier and downstream proton pumping that the shorter manuscript version omits, in addition to the apparent wording imprecision. Without independent evidence of the administered version, the potential influence of either feature on students' responses is **unknown**. No reanalysis of the retained 28/84 responses or ten pairs can resolve version provenance by itself.

## Current public copy audit and editorial decision

The main public AP Biology question is carefully introduced as **“Wording reproduced in the research manuscript”**. The excerpt describes binding **at the plastoquinone-binding site of PSII**, avoiding the inaccurate claim that Bentazon binds directly to the plastoquinone molecule. The schematic describes electron-transfer inhibition and two downstream consequences with the explicit qualifier that it is **one plausible pathway under substantial inhibition**, not proof of complete shutdown or plant death.

The adjacent **“What the pathway leaves open”** prose already identifies Bentazon as a PSII inhibitor while directly cautioning that isolated pea thylakoid experiments found **relatively weak inhibition and less specific Q_B interaction for Bentazon** than for other tested herbicides, citing Battaglino et al. (2021). It further distinguishes a test question's assumed inhibition from the strength or outcome of exposure in a specific plant.

Targeted review of the Action Research page, Research hub, homepage and general Teaching page found no other unsupported public statement that Bentazon **binds directly to plastoquinone**. The Action Research account discusses inhibited electron transport and the limits of inference from changing explanation prompts. The manuscript-attributed quote remains accurate *as a quote of that author's reproduced prompt*, without implying independent verification of the historically administered form.

**Editorial decision:** No public academic text changed. Replacing the manuscript quote with the longer private edited question would destroy source fidelity; adding a speculative disclaimer to an already qualified mechanism would create repetition without additional evidence. The source-level concern about a differently worded potential teaching instrument is preserved here for future original-record verification rather than silently attributed to all participating students.
