## Bang, Warren, Rosebery and Medin (2012)

**Original article:** [*Desettling Expectations in Science Education*](https://doi.org/10.1159/000345322), *Human Development*, **55(5–6), 302–318**, article first published online **7 January 2013**, in the 2012 print volume. Source access included [University of Washington's original journal-formatted PDF](https://education.uw.edu/sites/default/files/Bang%20Et%20A%20l2012HumanDev.pdf) and the publisher [original abstract](https://karger.com/hde/article-abstract/55/5-6/302/158112/Desettling-Expectations-in-Science-Education). The printed title page was visually inspected in the web PDF reader, along with relevant substantive original full-text passages.

**Original argument and evidence.** The paper addresses **settled expectations** in science education that delimit what is recognized as an adequate scientific explanation and who is positioned to participate. The authors juxtapose understandings of **life and water**, including a seventh-grade classification debate and varied relations between scientific and community knowledge, to invite reconsideration of an assumed nature–culture divide. The discussion is an interpretive and design-oriented analysis, not a universally representative student-outcomes experiment.

**Website claim:** “Later scholarship places such differences within cultural practices and examines the assumptions about nature that science education brings into classrooms (Bang & Medin, 2010; Bang et al., 2012).” **Accurate and proportionate.** The 2010 Bang–Medin paper was independently reviewed in C-RV23d; the 2012 paper adds a separate argument about norms embedded in classroom science. Keep the two distinct from **Bang, Medin & Atran (2007)**, which is the original fish-classification comparison in the same paragraph.

**Tier: F**, original article substantive text and first-page screenshot checked. **Bibliography retained at 2012** despite the online release in 2013.
