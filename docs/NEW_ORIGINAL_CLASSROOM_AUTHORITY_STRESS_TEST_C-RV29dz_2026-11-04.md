## New original Bae, Hogan and Hayes 2026 article

[Christine Lee Bae, Erin Hogan and Kathryn N. Hayes (2026), *Tensions in Fostering Student Epistemic Agency Through Sensemaking in Elementary Science*](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.70056), *Journal of Research in Science Teaching* **63, 550–568**, DOI **10.1002/tea.70056**, provided as the original full 19-page journal PDF in the user's Library. Read the abstract, conceptual framing, method and five-teacher sampling, qualitative case findings, post-lesson teacher reflections, discussion and limitations; visually inspected the original first published page.

The study analyses classroom video and post-lesson debriefs of **five elementary-school teachers identified as instructional innovators**. The authors identify tensions between opening space for student-generated ideas and providing science-disciplinary explanations, between useful scaffolds and restrictions on inquiry, between students' criteria for knowledge products and curricular disciplinary criteria, and between students' challenges to classroom rules and the demands of orderly teaching. The cases demonstrate that **affirming student contributions alone does not establish that students retain authority over the trajectory of the investigation**, and that scaffolding can both enable and constrain students' epistemic agency.

These new findings are consistent with the already published `research.html#curriculum-decisions` sequence. The passage moves from a student hypothetical about receptor signalling to teachers' noticing, how a contribution may be treated either as an answer or a question, the need for disciplinary resources, and finally judgments about legitimacy and authority. The website neither promises unconditional student control nor treats disciplinary correction as inherently unjust. Bae et al.'s five deliberately selected **elementary** teachers do not establish results in the author's own **secondary** science classroom; adding a generalizing secondary-learning outcome would exceed the article.

## New original Thorsen, Madsen and Holmegaard 2025 article

[Gry E. Thorsen, Lene Møller Madsen and Henriette T. Holmegaard (2025), *The (re)production of insecurities: an ethnographic study of the gendered science classroom in upper secondary school*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11422-025-10252-8), *Cultural Studies of Science Education* **20, 293–319**, DOI **10.1007/s11422-025-10252-8**, supplied in an original open-access 27-page PDF. Read the original abstract, theory, methods, teacher-student interaction examples, student interviews, interpretive discussion and conclusions.

The article draws on a **three-year ethnographic study in three Danish upper-secondary schools**, with observations from physics, chemistry and technology classes and interviews with participating students and teachers. Its micro-analysis concerns how teaching expectations and gendered norms can shape whose performances are treated as competent science participation. The study does not investigate biological sex development, chromosome classification or the effect of a particular inclusive secondary biology lesson.

The original offers a useful challenge to the website's statement that recognition involves judgments about who is viewed as capable of scientific work (`research.html#recognition-contexts`). No exaggeration was found: the public paragraph makes a bounded connection between **separate** Gonsalves doctoral physics identity research and Carlone/Johnson longitudinal trajectories, without suggesting all classrooms or all genders have identical experiences. The subsequent discussion of biological variation also preserves the distinction between **scientific adequacy of a representation** and **teacher responses to a student's challenge**. Inserting Thorsen as though it established a clinical biology claim would be a category error.
