## Original-source claim and reference checks

### Barrett (2013), professional identity and activist science teaching

- **Public claim:** "A longitudinal study followed an early-career teacher negotiating an activist commitment alongside the demands of school practice."
- **Evidence:** [Publisher's article page](https://ejrsme.icrsme.com/article/view/11994) and **original 20-page publisher-hosted PDF** [available through the same page](https://ejrsme.icrsme.com/article/view/11994/8435), title-page image and methods text inspected. The study follows one teacher through his first five years in an alternative publicly funded high school, discussing aspirations to teach for social justice, professional culture, workload, identity, and the difference between espoused and enacted beliefs. The source's observational status and one-teacher sample are faithfully conveyed.
- **Bibliographic nuance independently resolved:** The current journal *landing-page heading* truncates the title to "Becoming an Activist Science Teacher: A Longitudinal Case Study". **The printed PDF's title page**, and original ERIC archive's PDF, show "**Becoming an Activist Science Teacher: A Longitudinal Case Study of an Induction Intervention**". This is precisely the full title already given on the website. **Do not shorten the existing bibliography to match metadata that omits part of the paper title.** The printed PDF title page was independently screenshot-checked.

### Puvirajah, Verma and Webb (2012), informal robotics science

- **Public claim:** "Students and mentors participating in a robotics competition negotiated authority as they developed scientific and technical knowledge together."
- **Evidence:** [Original publisher *Cultural Studies of Science Education* page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11422-012-9394-2), 7, 375–408, and published manuscript/PDF [hosted in the author-uploaded record](https://scispace.com/pdf/examining-the-mediation-of-power-in-a-collaborative-ua73qp1o6f.pdf). Original journal-formatted first page visually screenshot-checked. Researchers used critical discourse analysis of competition dialogue and interviews with high school participants and mentors. They analyzed language, a growing shared sense of ownership, negotiated power, co-construction and critiques of knowledge claims in a **robotics competition**, which differed from conventional classrooms.
- **Disposition:** Accurate and appropriately context-specific. The source's account of *informal* learning is not misrepresented as a controlled study of classroom curriculum, nor does the website imply that the present author's students participated.

### Engle and Conant (2002), productive disciplinary engagement

- **Public claim:** "Productive disciplinary engagement provides a further account of how student authority can develop alongside responsibility to peers and access to disciplinary resources."
- **Evidence:** [Original *Cognition and Instruction* journal abstract](https://doi.org/10.1207/S1532690XCI2004_1), volume 20(4), 399–483, with [ERIC original indexed article record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ665018). Its **four linked design principles** are problematizing disciplinary subject matter, student intellectual authority, accountability to peer contributions and disciplinary standards, and necessary informational/material resources. Original empirical episode concerned **Grade 5 students' sustained controversy over species classification**.
- **Disposition:** Exact match with conceptual principles; site wisely does not pretend that authority alone is sufficient or that the article demonstrates efficacy for every population. Journal metadata lists online access posted in 2010 but **2002 print volume** remains correctly cited.

### Slotta and Linn (2009), web-supported inquiry

- **Public claim:** "Web-based science inquiry environments raise a related question about how the resources prepared for a lesson shape the explanations students develop."
- **Evidence:** [Original ERIC book record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED527596), ISBN and 216-page record, and [Teachers College Press book metadata as represented by Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/WISE_Science.html?id=jFrFngEACAAJ). *WISE Science: Web-Based Inquiry in the Classroom* discusses curriculum projects, scaffolds and teacher guidance for web-based science inquiry in **grades 6–12**. It describes research from a multi-year researcher–teacher design community, not a single controlled study.
- **Disposition:** The website **poses a pedagogical planning question**, not a specific experimental result, so the book description sufficiently supports the statement. The publisher's **complete 216-page book was not available for independent full-text review**, and no more precise chapter-level claim is marked verified. Existing title and ERIC link match the original index.
