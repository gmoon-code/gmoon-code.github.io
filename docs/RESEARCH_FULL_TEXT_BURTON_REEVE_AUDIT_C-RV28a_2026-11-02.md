## The concrete public factual issue discovered and repaired

The earlier `research.html#multilingual-participation` paragraph said:

> Classroom exchanges present a different interpretive challenge. During spoken-word-poetry activities in an academic English programme, multilingual students discussed racism amid emotional tensions that shaped their interactions (Burton, 2024).

The first of Burton's two core interactions was **a post-semester research interview**, which the original text explicitly reports. Treating both examples as classroom conversations during the activities obscured the nature and timing of the evidence. It could also make the reader infer the whole group of students discussed racism during classroom activities when this particular article analyzes two selected situations.

The two sentences were precisely replaced with:

> Conversations with students present a different interpretive challenge. In a Canadian university English-language programme, tensions around racism emerged during a post-semester interview and a private conversation about a student's poem, both examined in a study of a spoken-word-poetry curriculum (Burton, 2024).

This wording retains the author's sentence-to-sentence intellectual progression from tests and acculturation to questions about interpretation of educator-student interactions, but gives the research setting, source method and actual episode timing without an unwarranted generalization. The following sentences about both sources concerning **university** language learning and their possible relevance to **secondary science** were preserved. The revision follows Canadian English, does not use em dashes or inflated results, and changes no other page prose.
