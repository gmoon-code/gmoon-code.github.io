### AP Biology mechanisms and evidence

- **Battaglino, Grinzato and Pagliano (2021)**, *Binding Properties of Photosynthetic Herbicides with the QB Site of the D1 Protein in Plant Photosystem II*, *Plants* 10(8), 1501, [original open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8398153/), DOI `10.3390/plants10081501`. The experiment used isolated pea thylakoids and compared herbicides, finding relatively low inhibitory potency and weak/specificity-limited effects for bentazon relative to tested alternatives. The website accurately avoids inferring plant-level absorbed dose, response duration or certain death from the photosystem II mechanism.
- **Russ, Scherr, Hammer and Mikeska (2008)**, *Recognizing Mechanistic Reasoning in Student Scientific Inquiry*, [publisher original article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sce.20264), *Science Education* 92(3), 499–525. Its mechanistic analysis concerns first-grade discourse and frames entities/activities/relationships, not a study of this teacher's AP Biology cohort. The teaching page cites its **framework** and then explicitly says the criteria were adapted to the particular photosynthesis task.
- **Shiroda et al. (2024)**, *Examining and Supporting Mechanistic Explanations Across Chemistry and Biology Courses*, *CBE—Life Sciences Education* 23(3), ar38, [original open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11440742/), DOI `10.1187/cbe.23-08-0157`. This research examined **seven undergraduate introductory and upper-level chemistry/biology courses**, not the author's secondary classroom. The page uses the mechanistic framework without misrepresenting Shiroda's participant population or treatment effects. Preserve the actual em dash in the **published journal title**, which is exempt from the authored-prose punctuation rule.
- The University of Minnesota Extension source on photosystem II inhibitor herbicides is background information, and cannot establish dose-specific plant outcomes on its own. The page correctly notes possible membrane injury, ROS pathways and different effects of exposure and plant susceptibility.

### AP Chemistry representations and feedback

- **Johnstone (1991)**, *Why is science difficult to learn? Things are seldom what they seem*, *Journal of Computer Assisted Learning* 7(2), 75–83, DOI `10.1111/j.1365-2729.1991.tb00230.x`. The source's broad levels of chemical representation inform the site's macroscopic/particulate/symbolic lesson, without implying a measured effect in the author's classroom.
- **Talanquer (2011 issue; originally circulated online earlier)**, *Macro, submicro, and symbolic: The many faces of the chemistry “triplet”*, *International Journal of Science Education* 33(2), 179–195, [journal original article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09500690903386435). The account of different yet linked representational levels is properly used as disciplinary conceptual background. The website's publication-year reference matches the journal issue.
- **Black and Wiliam (2009)**, *Developing the Theory of Formative Assessment*, *Educational Assessment, Evaluation and Accountability* 21, 5–31, DOI `10.1007/s11092-008-9068-5`. Used in a prospective feedback plan; the site does not claim the constructed student's answer was observed or that an intervention improved outcomes. The exact numerical calculations independently passed in C-RV10.

### AP evolution and haemoglobin variation

- **Piel et al. (2010)**, *Global Distribution of the Sickle Cell Gene and Geographical Confirmation of the Malaria Hypothesis*, [original open Nature Communications paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1104) and [PMC full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3060623/), 1, article 104. The source reports its strongest geographical relationship in Africa, unresolved confirmation for Asia and the Americas, and uses historical allele frequency/endemicity sources. The page's regional and historical-data qualifications are faithful.
- **Williams et al. (2005)** original Kenyan-cohort protection estimates were verified in C-RV3: approximately 50% for mild clinical malaria, 75% for hospital admission, nearly 90% for severe/complicated outcomes and no established protection against symptomless parasitaemia. These clinical endpoints are not treated as population-selection coefficients.
- **Donovan (2022)** genetic-essentialism article is described as conceptual/pedagogical evidence about educational framing, without equating racial designations to individual genotypes. CDC's sickle-cell information and the HHMI BioInteractive teaching storyline are attributed for their respective health/educational roles rather than passed off as original data from the classroom.
