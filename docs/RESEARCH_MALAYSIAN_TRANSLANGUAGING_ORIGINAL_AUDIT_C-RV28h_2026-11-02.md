## Precise sample, data and findings

- The article follows **one Grade 5 English-as-a-second-language class** in a Malaysian Tamil national-type primary school in **Selangor**, with **31 Malaysian-Indian learners** (19 girls and 12 boys), mostly from upper-middle-class families, ages 10–11. Their home language was Tamil, and they studied Malay and English as additional languages. **The English-only rule concerned the English teacher's own lessons**, not a proven uniform policy governing every school subject.
- The author reports a **six-month naturalistic case study**. The teacher continued instructing students to use English during class and group work, while the researcher observed the peer interactions. The original article reports **50 video-recorded group activity sessions**, peer-group interviews with all 31 learners and a sociocultural critical discourse analysis (SCDA) of collaborative speech and interviews. The article says it coded **4,389 speech acts** across those transcripts, generating and organizing descriptions of functions that translanguaging served.
- Observed discourse and learner accounts showed strategic language switching for peer support and collaborative meaning-making, sometimes to maintain rapport, resolve disagreements or make connections across languages. The article also documents constraints arising from the teacher's and peers' language expectations, participants' reports of parental attitudes to English as economic capital and regional cultural/ethnic dynamics.
- The descriptive evidence does **not** establish a randomized causal effect of translanguaging on assessment outcomes, nor does it measure **secondary science understanding**. The website explicitly acknowledges the different subject, age and educational setting.

**Important source provenance separation.** The author's [broader doctoral project summary](https://www.shakinarajendram.com/translanguaging) describes **55 learners across two Grade 5 classrooms** and two teachers. That project overlaps with, but is not identical to, the **31-learner, one-classroom** article. The thesis-wide 55 or 100 video-transcript description must not silently replace the 31-person journal article's population and 50-recording data. The website does not provide either number, so no reader-facing numerical revision is needed.
