## Identity of the original 2009 book and pagination reconciliation

- [Original WISE research group's publication index](https://wise-research.berkeley.edu/publications/) lists **James D. Slotta and Marcia C. Linn (2009)**, *WISE Science: Web-Based Inquiry in the Classroom*, Teachers College Press, paperback ISBN **978-0-8077-4949-4**.
- [ERIC ED527596](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED527596) lists the April 2009 book, **216 pages**, record type **Non-Journal** and **Abstractor: As Provided**, ISBN **0-8077-4950-8** for the hardback. Its publisher-supplied synopsis describes free customizable web-based curriculum projects, teacher supports and assessments for **Grades 6–12**, based on a multi-year curriculum/research partnership. This **publisher-authored synopsis** explains the book's scope, but is not a chapter-level source or original measured outcomes.
- Two [Google Books bibliographic records](https://books.google.com/books/about/WISE_Science.html?id=jFrFngEACAAJ) and [hardback edition record](https://books.google.com/books/about/WISE_Science.html?id=r1QkAQAAMAAJ) report **216 and 202 pages** respectively. The [Vermont library catalog](https://catalog.libraries.vsc.edu/cgi-bin/koha/opac-detail.pl?biblionumber=316450&shelfbrowse_itemnumber=431418) explicitly gives **xiv, 202 pages**, and [CiNii library holdings](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BB05470556) independently confirms **xiv, 202 pages**. Thus 216 = 14 Roman-numbered preliminary pages + 202 consecutively numbered body pages. These catalogues reconcile the previous register's apparent 202/216 mismatch rather than casting doubt on the edition.
- Library contents list **Teaching and learning science in the 21st century; WISE: the Web-based Inquiry Science Environment; The knowledge integration approach; Instructional patterns for knowledge integration; Designing WISE curriculum: a partnership approach; What do students learn from WISE?; Teaching with WISE; WISE professional development; Toward the classroom of the future**. Google Books shows select contents headings, keyword lists and **snippet-view** classification. These do **not** establish reading original complete 2009 chapter argument.

## Editorial review and style

The five-paragraph sequence proceeds from what teachers notice and may invite, to planned opportunities for epistemic agency, to the **specific resources and disciplinary supports** needed when a student question changes the investigation. An observation about physical materials leads meaningfully to the WISE question about prepared web resources, and the concluding sentence reintroduces the balance between planning and openness. This is a concrete conceptual relationship without mechanical “Furthermore” transitions or forced corrective contrast. The following paragraph shifts from lesson materials to authority in schools and informal robotics, then moves to claims of rightful presence and the legitimacy of student contributions. The teacher's agency and the student's contribution remain explicit, with a nuanced distinction between recognition and curricular influence.

The cited WISE sentence's modal question does not pretend an experimental comparison was carried out in this teacher's lessons. A rewrite adding pupil-learning effects would exceed the inspected original source description, while a rewrite solely to vary diction would diminish the author's natural, thoughtful voice. All five paragraphs have purposeful steps, varied grammatical structure, no em dash or repetitive stock transitional opening. **No public `research.html` change warranted**.
