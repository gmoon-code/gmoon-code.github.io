## Actual research design and warrant

The study came from a larger collaboratively designed unit in a **US seventh-grade science classroom**, with a biology-focused unit on community stress. The original published article describes a **19-student Spring 2022 classroom pilot**, and situates its analysis in two classroom episodes from community survey development, using interaction analysis of audiovisual classroom records, field notes and slides. In the **first episode**, youth and adults considered a proposed income question, privacy, the possible stress or discomfort it could cause respondents, and whether to remove it. In the **second**, they examined a pilot survey's gender question and considered how available response choices affected gender-minoritized respondents. The analysis argues that **care for survey takers** emerged through these negotiations as an *epistemic ideal*, with youths' and adults' participation shaped by relations of power and affect. These are **situated interactions and authors' interpretations**, not proof of a general improvement in science achievement or an intervention implemented in the website author's classroom.

The current public `research.html`, Git blob `f083527f1cc0f8d60587133333b15ccc0d994237`, reads:

> A community survey designed during a seventh-grade biology unit on stress illustrates how scientific and ethical judgments can develop together. Students questioned the effects their questions might have on respondents, and adults and youth negotiated care for those respondents as part of deciding what would count as a responsible inquiry (Krishnamoorthy et al., 2026).

This wording is **faithful to the two episode analyses** and careful about inferred consequences. The article's original methodological focus on survey creation and revision supports both sentences. The website does not exaggerate sample representativeness, claim all students questioned both items, treat interpreted care as a directly measured individual trait, or generalize a US middle-school case into its author's secondary biology cohort. The two-sentence sidebar complements rather than repeats the adjacent paragraphs on teacher noticing, disciplinary authority and rightful presence. It gives a specific concrete case of youth contesting a potential research practice, leaving the ensuing research-interest question in the author's restrained voice.
