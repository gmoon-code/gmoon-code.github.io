The accessible publisher abstract explicitly places the discussion in **Canadian public schools** responding to human-rights protections for gender identity/expression, and draws on **Claudia Ruitenberg's writing on a Derridean ethic of hospitality**. ## Claim-by-claim and sentence-by-sentence decisions

1. **Teacher assumptions.** The first sentence asks teachers to reflect on which students they imagine they already know. It follows the earlier paragraph's attention to the authority of classification, while moving from clinical/policy evidence into an explicitly different school-ethical question. It is the website author's educational framing, not an empirical finding attributed to Airton.
2. **Ethic of hospitality.** The second sentence closely follows the publisher's source-supplied abstract. Teachers are invited to prepare for students whose gender cannot be anticipated, without limiting their obligations to students already recognized as transgender. No causal claim about teaching outcomes or a specific identity group is introduced. The publisher's fuller three-orientation formulation remains in the source; the website's selective paraphrase does not overstate it.
3. **Gilbert's expertise critique.** The third sentence is a separate conceptual case, already vetted in C-RV23b and C-RV28r. Its connection to Airton is meaningful through the question of adults' assumptions about students. It does not pretend that either article reports a trial of a secondary biology lesson.

The seven-paragraph local development retains a coherent sequence. Scientific developmental mechanisms precede representation; an historical misuse of XYY data leads to medical/sport classification and the consequences of institutional judgement; then teacher assumptions, adult expertise and participation in non-classroom settings are clearly distinguished. The final paragraph returns to a tractable classroom question about how teachers respond when students challenge a model. The sentence structures and transitions are varied; an additional formulaic connector, a general moralising conclusion, or a forced link between distinct study populations would weaken the author's restrained academic voice.

**Editing decision:** No public `research.html` change. The paragraph and citation are sufficiently faithful, appropriately qualified, cohesive, natural in Canadian academic English and consistent with the author's established no-em-dash and low-repetition preferences. **Evidence tier remains A**, with no claim of reading a restricted article cover to cover.
