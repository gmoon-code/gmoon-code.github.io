## Scientific and editorial interpretation

The question asks students to explain a possible cancer pathway in which phosphatase dysfunction changes the phosphorylation state of downstream signalling proteins. That is a legitimate **possible mechanism**. It is too broad to warrant a universal claim that defective phosphatases always increase signalling or necessarily cause cancer, because phosphatases can have tumour-suppressive, oncogenic or context-dependent functions. For example, original [Tartaglia et al. (2003)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12717436/) documented **gain-of-function** PTPN11 mutations in some myeloid malignancies; the mechanistic [protein tyrosine phosphatase review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5413367/) distinguishes tumour-suppressive from oncogenic phosphatases; the [phosphatase review in cancer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33827975/) contrasts oncogenic SHP2 and tumour-suppressive PP2A.

Re-fetched `research-action.html` and the related `research.html`, `teaching.html`, `index.html` and `cv.html` sources. The Action Research page describes the Unit 4 task in careful terms, as **“how altered phosphatase regulation could affect cell signalling and cancer-related growth.”** It reports **25/28 complete classifications** under **Unit 4's own criteria** and explicitly says that the different biological mechanism prevents measuring progress on a common scale. None of the related sources asserts that *all* phosphatase dysfunction causes cancer, that the same response complexity was assessed across units, or that the edited Unit 4 PDF has independently authenticated its own use.

**Editorial decision:** No public academic HTML changes. Replacing the careful, accurate Action Research sentence with the unqualified student-facing question would degrade scientific precision, while adding a new disclaimer about every phosphatase subtype would repeat limitations that are not needed for the page's purpose. Keep the question as historical manuscript-reported wording and the full provenance issue in the private/public audit record, rather than inserting unsupported certainty into the academic website.
