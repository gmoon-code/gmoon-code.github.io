## Independent subsequent-review evidence

**Re and Birkhoff:** Laura Re and Jutta M. Birkhoff, “The 47,XYY syndrome, 50 years of certainties and doubts: A systematic review”, *Aggression and Violent Behavior* **22**, 9–17, **2015**, DOI **10.1016/j.avb.2015.02.003**. The original [Elsevier article record and highlights](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1359178915000348), plus the [University of Insubria research archive](https://irinsubria.uninsubria.it/handle/11383/1998321), explicitly report that reviewed studies do not establish a statistically supported XYY-specific predisposition to violent/deviant behaviour. Its discussion also distinguishes a genetic substrate from treating an extra chromosome as sufficient explanation of an individual action. The site accurately says **“by itself”** and locates the absence of statistically demonstrated predisposition in the 2015 review, rather than as an empirical finding of Richardson's 2013 historical book.

Avoid stronger statements that no measured associations have *ever* existed, that all people with XYY share one behavioural profile, or that an additional Y chromosome cannot influence any phenotype. The actual claim is about **unjustified behavioural prediction from the chromosome alone**. The historical book's discussion of past research practices and the later review's survey of evidence serve distinct inferential purposes.

## Editorial, paragraph and voice decisions

The first sentence raises the issue of **what use is made of a biological classification**, carrying the earlier model-representation discussion into an historical case. The second then provides Richardson's distinct history of gendered scientific interpretation; the third tests the stronger behavioural inference against a later review of the evidence; the fourth draws a careful interpretation of what went wrong. These four sentences move naturally from methodological question to documented example, evidential limitation and bounded consequence. There is no inflated moral conclusion, repeated generic “This suggests”, unnecessary em dash or formulaic connector to revise.

The following paragraph shifts from XYY-based behavioural claims to decisions about diagnostic categories, hormone measurements and dated sport-eligibility policies. Their common thread is the consequences of **inference from biological measures**, but the sources and settings remain separate. The following teacher-assumptions and participation paragraphs are not claimed as experimentally caused outcomes of these histories. The original site respects Canadian-English “behaviour”, separates the two distinct studies by citation and avoids an unqualified claim about contemporary sex development or educational intervention.
