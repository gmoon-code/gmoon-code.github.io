## Original article and distinct source checks

The [original Taylor & Francis article abstract/metadata](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14675986.2015.1109775) and its [journal issue](https://www.tandfonline.com/toc/ceji20/26/6) identify **Gail Prasad**, “Beyond the mirror towards a plurilingual prism: Exploring the creation of plurilingual ‘identity texts’ in English and French classrooms in Toronto and Montpellier”, *Intercultural Education* **26(6), 497–514**, DOI **10.1080/14675986.2015.1109775**. The paper belongs to the **2015 issue**, while the publisher records **online publication on 1 February 2016**. [EBSCO's original indexed journal record](https://openurl.ebsco.com/results?bquery=AU+Prasad%2C+Gail) independently lists **2015, volume 26, issue 6**. An older [author-associated publication list](https://www.iamplurilingual.com/publications.html) gives a conflicting provisional-looking **2016, volume 27(1)** for the same DOI; the final publisher record and indexed issue resolve this discrepancy without a website edit.

The publisher's abstract specifies **exploratory comparative and collaborative ethnographic action research** in **four English/French school cases in Toronto and one in Montpellier**, with children participating as co-researchers of their plurilingual lives. The article analyses the iterative creation of **plurilingual multimodal books**, examining **one plurilingual identity text from each of five cases**, children's research conversations about their creations, and interviews with teachers and parents. Its intended inference concerns creative inclusive plurilingual pedagogy in **language/literacy schooling**. The study is not a controlled experiment or an empirical test of students' secondary science explanations.

The author's [doctoral-project abstract](https://www.iamplurilingual.com/abstract.html) independently corroborates the five-school design and describes photo-elicitation, collaborative reflection, drawing, creative writing and collage. In addition, the original 2015 University of Toronto dissertation, *The Prism of Children's Plurilingualism: A Multi-site Inquiry with Children as Co-researchers across English and French Schools in Toronto and Montpellier*, is text-accessible through [Library and Archives Canada](https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item?app=Library&id=TC-OTU-71458&oclc_number=1032965678&op=pdf). In the dissertation's methodology, section 3.3.4 describes collaboratively determined plurilingual multimodal books, with printed/electronic illustrations and recordings of readings, and section 3.4 explains both spontaneous and guided **research conversations**. Some recordings were difficult to decipher because of competing classroom voices; observation notes and guided discussions were therefore additional records. Section 3.5 describes interviews with classroom teachers and parents. This authentic **underlying dissertation** supports the broader project context and shows why written/visual artefacts alone cannot capture every contribution, **but it is a distinct work and cannot be substituted for verification of the exact 2015 journal article's methods and findings**.

The original publisher article's `/full/` route was restricted in direct retrieval (HTTP 403), and [ResearchGate's exact-paper record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/292670347_Beyond_the_mirror_towards_a_plurilingual_prism_exploring_the_creation_of_plurilingual_%27identity_texts%27_in_English_and_French_classrooms_in_Toronto_and_Montpellier) offers **Request Full-text**, not the main text. Searches for an institutional or accepted article manuscript did not yield an independently authenticated copy of this exact journal work.
