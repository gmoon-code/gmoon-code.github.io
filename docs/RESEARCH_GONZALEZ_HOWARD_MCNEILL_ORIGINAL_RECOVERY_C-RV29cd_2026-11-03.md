## Original methods and results directly related to the website

The study investigated critique in argumentation seminars in **three middle-school classrooms**. The researchers used **social network analysis** to map patterns of critique among participants and **discourse analysis** of surrounding language moves to examine how teachers and students created or constrained opportunities for critique. Both researchers treated epistemic agency as socially negotiated rather than an automatic property of any statement labelled argumentation.

Actual published results include the heterogeneity of participation. Some students engaged repeatedly in critiquing peers, while other students in the focal groups did not offer or receive an observed critique. The researchers did not treat classroom argumentation as equally redistributed authority for all learners. Their analyses of classroom moves describe how teacher clarification and normalization of dissent could make participation in critique available and how students sometimes challenged peers' claims or evidence.

The original discussion presents the work as exploratory, situated and limited by focal classroom interactions. These findings support a **possibility/manifestation** claim about student epistemic agency during peer discussion, not an intervention effectiveness estimate or a universal claim that all pupils who argue possess equal agency.
