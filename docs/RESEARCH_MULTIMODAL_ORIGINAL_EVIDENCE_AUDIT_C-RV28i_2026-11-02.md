## Source 1 | Tang, Delgado and Moje (2014), relevant original article text reviewed

Bibliographic identity independently matches both the [original University of Michigan institutional-repository PDF](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/106155/sce21099.pdf), preserved in published *Science Education* layout, and [Wiley publisher article record](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.21099). Relevant original PDF text was read directly from the institutional-repository copy. Original methods, units of analysis, student artefacts, excerpts and interpretations were inspected, including the underlying distinctions between representations and their semiotic components. A **page-zero image screenshot was attempted**, but the web screenshot service returned **HTTP 403** for the repository binary. **No successful PDF page-image check is claimed**. The full original article's extracted text is accessible and bears the published title/author/DOI/journal headers; institutional hosting and publisher record corroborate the publication identity. Figure-image fidelity, PDF-byte identity and complete figure review remain unverified.

The paper proposes **two analytically independent dimensions**, *timescale* and *compositional grain size*. Examination of transformations **between or across representations**, commonly over longer timescales and treating entire artefacts as units, is characteristic of multiple-representations analysis. Examination of how **words, diagrams, gestures and other modalities are combined to make meaning within representations**, commonly at shorter timescales and finer compositional grain size, is characteristic of multimodal analysis. Crucially, the proposed dimensions are **not a rigid binary**. The authors explicitly describe other combinations of long/short timescale and fine/large granularity and emphasize how analyses complement each other.

The illustrative case involved **40 volunteers from two US middle schools** in a free **two-week summer programme**, with six lessons in a project strand on size and scale/nanoscience. Video, fieldnotes, instructional materials and student-produced representations were used. The paper describes video-based comparisons of a focal pseudonymous three-student group, including how top- and side-view diagrams of surface texture, students' verbal accounts and gestures permitted different explanations. Researchers followed sequences of representations across lessons, then used finer-grained linguistic, visual and gestural analysis of selected episodes. The authors used the framework **retrospectively** and call for future research on prospective curriculum use. These findings cannot be generalized to all secondary biology students or construed as experimental evidence of a particular intervention's benefits.

### Exact public claim and writing decision

The current `research.html` paragraph says:

> An analytic framework distinguishes changes across representations from the ways learners combine words, gestures, and visual elements within a representation (Tang et al., 2014).

The sentence accurately describes the article's **two archetypal analytical foci**, without stating they are mutually exclusive or that the framework is a proven treatment. The original source's complementary timescale and compositional-granularity dimensions are real, but adding both to this short sentence is unnecessary for the paragraph's purpose. The preceding sentence concerns the different language resources bilingual learners use across elementary-classroom participation structures. The following sentences discuss linguistic and visual contributions to explaining electric flow and the instructor's mediation in a **separate** out-of-school science study. The framework sentence functions as a conceptual bridge without turning these different settings into a common intervention or causal account. The paragraph's surrounding transitions, varied syntactic movement and personal voice remain appropriate.

**Disposition:** Tang et al. (2014), rolling-source row **50**, advances from **A** to **F**, under the established meaning of **substantial original text actually reviewed**. A page-image screenshot did not succeed and must not be reported as if it did. No public prose change warranted.
