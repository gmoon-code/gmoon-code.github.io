## S03 Fine (2022) | Actual empirical warrant

1. **Research question / unit of study.** Fine explicitly asked how an English-dominant **science teacher** developed the capacity to interpret multilingual learners' science ideas on translanguaging-welcoming formative assessments. The instrumental case is the teacher's interpretive development within a teacher-researcher co-design collaborative, **not** a sample estimating science-learning gains or a direct comparison of students' concept scores.
2. **Main analyzed evidence.** The author depended primarily on transcripts from **eight 60–90-minute teacher–researcher Reflect and Modify meetings**, supplemented by interviews, formative-assessment samples, and collaborative records. Coded episodes trace how the teachers and researcher made sense of students' work. In the original article's terms, student artifacts were also used to triangulate the interpretation of teachers' changing practice.
3. **Student scientific evidence is genuinely present.** The first cycle examined Spanish-language written exit tickets about **air, mass and volume**, with the group's oral translation and resorting of a previously uninterpreted student response based on recognizable science relationships. The second examined bilingual written observations/questions about **air, oxygen and water displacement**, where language clarification changed the teachers' interpretation of the observations. These are analyzable instances in which student performance was interpreted in relation to a science target.
4. **The evidence route changes across cycles.** The third cycle included a **multimodal group video about conservation of mass**. Teacher interpretation focused partly on molecular models and other visual elements when Spanish oral explanations were difficult to understand. The fourth cycle addressed **oral talk and posters about ecosystem decomposers**, including distinctions in everyday Spanish ecological terms. It is false to describe the entire study as relying only on student written responses.
5. **Eligibility decision.** The complete source strengthens a **qualified inclusion rationale** in a scoping review concerned with the *validity of interpreting student science performances*: teacher analysis of multilingual student work, with concrete disciplinary examples, is not merely detached policy commentary. At the same time, because Fine's own primary dependent inquiry concerns **teacher interpretive development**, the case remains an **edge of the stated 'direct science-understanding claim' eligibility rule**. The source does not report an independently established change in students' understanding and must not be cited as such.
6. **Dominant route adjudication.** The July original workbook classified S03 as **interactional**; the August DOI-associated preprint coded it **written/task response**, which can reasonably refer to the **kind of student work** at the centre of the early teacher-interpretation episodes. As a description of **the original article's primary analysis method**, however, written/task response would be misleading, since the directly analysed research evidence is teacher-researcher interaction. The August assignment is therefore a **reviewer-defined analytic judgment** that is *plausible but not independently necessary*, rather than an objectively verified property of Fine's methods. A clear row-level S03 coding memo should record this distinction and recognize the visual/oral secondary routes.

**Public editorial decision:** `research-scoping.html` already says the totals are the classifications *reported in the August preprint* and that studies may employ multiple evidence forms. This wording is careful enough; no additional correction to preprint-reported totals or unsourced eligibility claim is warranted solely because Fine's design is mixed.

## Learner-profile original-source checks

**S02 Uddling and Reath Warren (2023):** The open-access [original article, *A newcomer's spontaneous translanguaging in lower-secondary physics education*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19313152.2023.2208508) explicitly recounts the focal student's **schooling in Spain**, approximately **six months in an English school in the UK**, and **two years in Sweden** by the observation date. It also contrasts this with the peer participant's schooling entirely in Sweden. This is an unambiguous source-level prior-schooling description and gives **one separately corroborated** member of the August manuscript's three reported prior-schooling cases. This study is also the traceable **newcomer-status** case. The original article's actual classroom language was Swedish, while the two students' peer discussions used Swedish, English and other home languages. The page's claim about different performance language conditions is supported at example level.

**S04 Widing et al. (2022):** As already established in [C-RV21a](SCOPING_ROUTE_RECLASSIFICATION_AUDIT_C-RV21a_2026-11-02.md), the original polymer-chemistry source reports approximately **3–6 years** living and attending schools in Sweden among second-language participants. This provides a **second** directly corroborated case of prior schooling. Its before/after written chemistry concepts justify its August dominant written-response classification.

Together the original sources now corroborate **at least two** examples supporting a nonzero prior-schooling category. They **do not identify and verify the third case** or establish that the August criteria are applied consistently across the whole 20-study matrix. The August `3 of 20` total remains **author-manuscript corroborated**, not independently reconstructed from a final-August study-level chart. The July 2026 original workbook's older zero count was an earlier coding decision, now superseded for public alignment by the later manuscript; do not treat a historical coding difference as evidence that participants actually lacked prior schooling.

**Access boundaries:** The author's archived PDF copies of Uddling, Zhang, Swanson and Kang yielded no machine-readable content from the Files text reader. Uddling's open-access **HTML** article supplied original usable methods and participants text without OCR. Other possible third-case articles have not been marked verified from scraped abstracts alone. No OCR of private documents or reconstruction of unreported participant histories was attempted.
