## Scope, standards, and status definitions

Check all reader-facing text, including metadata, headings, links, alt text, disclosures, footnotes, examples, captions, scholarship, and claims. For each section evaluate evidence, analytical scope, clarity, sentence-to-sentence relationships, paragraph development, Canadian English, repeated ideas and terminology, and cross-page consistency. Preserve the author's restrained, reflective voice. Never add em dashes, prose colons, corrective contrasts, source-led filler, mechanical transitions, or rule-of-three rhetoric merely for polish. Maximize justified improvement, not changed word count.

Status terms: **Not reviewed in renewed pass**, **In progress**, **Reviewed with issues**, **Corrected but unverified**, **Editorially verified**, **Factually verified**, **Fully verified**, and **Blocked**. A section may be editorially verified while some claims remain cross-page-checked rather than independently verified from primary records. Full verification requires independent evidence for all factual claims and all other applicable checks.

## Stage C-TO1b | Arithmetic, osmotic mechanism and inference boundary

**Independent calculation.** For 0 g/100 mL, (10.8−10.0)/10.0 × 100 = **+8%**, a **+0.8 g** change. For 10 g/100 mL, (9.6−10.0)/10.0 × 100 = **−4%**, a **−0.4 g** change. For 20 g/100 mL, (8.7−10.0)/10.0 × 100 = **−13%**, a **−1.3 g** change. All values match the worksheet's original teacher-answer percentages; its student-facing formula is correct. A simple interpolation or absence of mass change after 30 minutes cannot establish the isotonic concentration of individual living cells. The three unreplicated observations allow no variance or reliability estimate.

**Scientific corroboration.** OpenStax *Biology 2e*, [5.2 Passive Transport](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/5-2-passive-transport) explains selective membrane permeability and osmosis; [30.5 Transport of Water and Solutes in Plants](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/30-5-transport-of-water-and-solutes-in-plants) specifies that net water movement follows the gradient of **total water potential**, influenced by solute and pressure potential (and sometimes other components). The original teacher notes mentioned a water-potential difference but left its direction implicit. The improved notes explicitly state that net water movement is from higher to lower water potential, while retaining that this is a possible **mechanism inferred** from a tissue-mass measurement. Weighing and blotting cannot directly establish flux across a specific membrane.
