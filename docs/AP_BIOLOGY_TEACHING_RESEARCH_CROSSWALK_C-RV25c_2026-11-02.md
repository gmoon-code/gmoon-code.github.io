## Empirical and hypothetical material remain distinct

The teaching case states that **28** students completed an original **two-point Unit 3 free-response question** concerning bentazon's inhibition of photosynthetic electron transport; subsequently, feedback-assisted correction occurred **without handing out a complete model causal pathway**. All 28 then undertook an **ungraded, individual 15-minute closed-resource reconstruction** two school days later under **a more explicit prompt**. The teacher later compared the **ten** original below-complete answers with their reconstructions after grades were finalized. Every relevant date interval, participant total and instructional condition agrees with `research-action.html`, and the teaching account does not claim the extra practice caused improved understanding.

The text distinguishes the **research coding categories** (nonmechanistic, partial, complete and separately observed contradictions) from the students' original **two-point assessment scores**. The unit-specific completeness standard allows a scientifically defensible *alternative causal pathway* if it explains the intermediate mechanism adequately. ## Original scientific-source verification

- **Bentazon limitation.** [Battaglino, Grinzato & Pagliano (2021), original open paper in *Plants*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8398153/), abstract and relevant methods/results were inspected. The study combined functional assays in **isolated pea thylakoids** and molecular docking; **bentazon displayed the weakest inhibition and least specific Q_B-pocket binding among the five tested herbicides**. The website accurately limits its statement to the tested conditions. It also distinguishes a question *assuming substantial inhibition* from demonstrating a particular concentration, organism's exposure or plant death. A herbicide may cause injury through additional processes, including oxidative injury, so a simple chain from diminished ATP/NADPH to plant death is **not** treated as uniquely necessary or experimentally established.
- **Mechanistic explanations.** [Shiroda et al. (2024), original open full article in *CBE—Life Sciences Education*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11440742/) describes explanatory categories according to entities, their activities and causal links. Its undergraduate multiscale tasks and categorization are **not** identical to the author's single AP Biology item and student responses. The website correctly explains it **developed item-specific criteria** and did not apply the publication's general scheme mechanically to students. This is especially important for alternative mechanistic pathways.
- **Chemistry and biology.** Electron transfer through PSII ordinarily supports the downstream proton gradient for ATP synthase and electron supply for NADPH production, while carbon fixation uses ATP and NADPH. The teaching page explicitly discusses both pathways and notes that actual injury and death cannot be inferred from those pathways alone without exposure and plant-level evidence. The page does not assert complete cessation of all photochemical reactions under any dose.
- **Other sources.** Russ et al. (2008) and University of Minnesota Extension are already cited on the page and their exact reference strings were inspected. This stage did **not** independently re-read all text of these sources or redo older source checks, so they remain within earlier verified scope rather than silently obtaining a higher evidence tier.
