## New source distinction for manuscript prompts and quoted texts

The late manuscript's **Appendix A** reproduces author-reported Unit 2, 3 and 4 target assessment prompts, **Appendix B** documents the item-specific classification criteria, **Appendix C** reproduces reflection, preparation and reconstruction instructions, and **Appendix D** reproduces practitioner note A–D text, including identifiable editorial additions in brackets. These provide **article-level protocol descriptions**, a useful richer source than a headline numerical summary. They are **not independent copies of the originally distributed student forms, timestamped notes or administrative records**. Their presence narrows the uncertainty about what the manuscript claims was asked, but does not independently establish historical instrument fidelity.

The article's Table 2 presents selected student-response excerpts as quotations and states that **original student grammar was retained**, with minor arrow normalization. The selected excerpts are not described by the manuscript as complete responses used for all classifications. The public website deliberately calls its case examples **paraphrases of the retained de-identified records**, which is a defensible distinct presentation choice. The existence of private **September-to-October changes to retained response strings**, previously documented in [C-RV29aq](ACTION_RESEARCH_TEXT_VERSION_PROVENANCE_AUDIT_C-RV29aq_2026-11-03.md) and [C-RV29ca](ACTION_RESEARCH_OCTOBER_WORKBOOK_VERSION_DELTA_C-RV29ca_2026-11-03.md), means that the **manuscript's literal-original-wording claim still requires a dated transcription/version history or source facsimiles** before this independent audit could authenticate it. This is a verification boundary, not evidence that the quotes are invented or altered improperly.

Likewise, a practitioner note being reproduced as A/B in an appendix does not independently authenticate its **contemporaneous date of creation**; the appendix supplies text and its author's sequence labels.
