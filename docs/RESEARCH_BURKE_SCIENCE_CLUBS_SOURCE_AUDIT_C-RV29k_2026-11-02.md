## Independent original publication checks

[Wiley's original article record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/tea.21714), [the November 2021 journal-issue record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/toc/10982736/2021/58/9) and [ERIC EJ1310767](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1310767) independently establish authors **Lydia E. Carol-Ann Burke and Ana Maria Navas Iannini**, title **“Science engagement as insight into the science identity work nurtured in community-based science clubs”**, *Journal of Research in Science Teaching* **58(9), 1425–1454**, DOI **10.1002/tea.21714**. Wiley identifies the version-of-record first-online date as **24 May 2021**, with issue date November 2021.

The authors' original published abstract reports the following separate sources and populations:

- **202 children's survey responses**, with children enrolled for at least one academic year at **21** out-of-school science club sites.
- Focus groups with a subset of **45** of those children attending **14** clubs. These **45** are not an additional independent sample to add to the **202**.
- Semistructured interviews with **nine** staff members and field notes after club promotional events. Staff interviews and promotional-event notes provide contextual evidence, not additional child-survey participants.
- Children of late-elementary through middle-school age in **low-income communities in an East-Central Canadian metropolitan area**. This is not a secondary-school biology intervention or a randomly assigned comparison of school grades.

The original abstract describes participants' and families' interest in science before club participation, enthusiasm for hands-on activities and positive relationships with staff. It also reports that **both children and staff often reproduced a rigid school/club distinction**. The authors observe that children *described themselves as improving* their school-science performance, while finding it difficult to relate club enjoyment to school learning. This is a contrast within the participants' accounts, **not independently measured achievement gains, causal evidence of club attendance improving school grades, or evidence that informal learning automatically transfers into classrooms**. The article proposes ways of strengthening the science connections between school, club and home.

## Source-to-sentence evaluation and writing-style decision

The paragraph's first sentence faithfully summarises participants' *reported* enjoyment of practical science activities and relationships. It would be more specific to say “club staff”, but the surrounding sentence's meaning is already clear and the existing phrasing does not overstate an evidential finding. The second sentence introduces the school–club distinction in a qualified “could be treated as separate” construction, allowing the abstract's finding about some children's and staff members' reinforcement of the distinction without treating every child as identical. It does not falsely say that attendance reduced school achievement. The third sentence is explicitly the author's **research question**. It considers whether setting boundaries shape the scientific work students feel able to pursue; it does not attribute this possible mechanism to a measured causal result in the study.

The neighbouring five-paragraph sequence moves from observed differences in spontaneously mentioned ecological relations, to other situated experiences and feelings, then to informal science clubs, recognition and researcher interpretation. Each paragraph provides a distinct evidentiary case and develops an identifiable connection to the next. The closing research question in the Burke paragraph leads naturally into the subsequent paragraph's discussion of how scientific identities are recognized by others, without repetitive “furthermore/this suggests” transitions. Canadian-English usage, understated first-person questioning and absence of em dashes follow the owner's style. **No source-driven factual or prose correction was warranted.** Rewriting it again after C-RV28j would violate the principle of maximum justified improvement rather than maximal rewriting.
