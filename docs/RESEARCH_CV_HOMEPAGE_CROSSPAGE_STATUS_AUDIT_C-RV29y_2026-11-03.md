## Action research, distinct explanatory measures and publication status

The CV lists **“Manuscript under review”** for *Reconsidering assessment evidence and practitioner judgment through classroom action research in biology*, without journal publication details or an acceptance claim. The action-research page calls it an **“Unpublished manuscript”**, which is **compatible**: a submitted manuscript may simultaneously be unpublished. However, the *current* under-review status depends on **editorial correspondence or the author's report** and was not rechecked against a journal account; source-level agreement does **not** verify that an editor is presently reviewing the manuscript.

The Research overview identifies one **28-student** AP Biology class, two feedback-related adjustments and **ten** selected Unit 3 answer/reconstruction pairs. The CV likewise reports **28** and ten same-mechanism response pairs, while its bullets distinguish written explanations, reflections, preparation records and practitioner notes. The action page gives the original assessment's **18/28 complete** Unit 3 responses, alongside **7/28 Unit 2** and **25/28 Unit 4**, and clearly warns that the three distinct questions do not measure a single invariant ability or provide a causal longitudinal treatment estimate.

The homepage's detailed spotlight states **five extended pathways, two earlier contradictions not repeated, two omitted relationships and one persistently undeveloped**, totaling ten. Its S20 account identifies an original explanation connecting weaker proton pumping, proton gradient, ATP and NADPH, with these relationships not developed in the later reconstruction. The Research hub likewise states the earlier-vs-later omission without alleging loss of knowledge. Original and reconstructed answers followed feedback and an explicitly more demanding prompt, so the descriptions do **not** justify claiming reconstruction produced a specific causal knowledge gain. The website's language remains conditional.

The overview explicitly states the follow-up questions formulated from the paired evidence **were not administered**. The CV says reconstruction informed *questions for subsequent assessment* and **“leaving the adjustment's effects on understanding unresolved”**, consistent with proposed rather than delivered follow-up assessment. The home spotlight links to the paired evidence and does not imply these retrospective questions were tested.

**Primary record boundary:** The public analysis and previous [C-RV22 manuscript comparison](ACTION_RESEARCH_SOURCE_AUDIT_C-RV22_2026-11-02.md) confirm manuscript-to-website coherence, not independent validation of the original de-identified participant answers, item rubrics, re-ratings, second reader's assessment or consent/assent/director authorization. Those confidential original records remain unavailable for independent source examination here. No classroom or journal material was invented to fill the gap.

## Editing, rhetoric and cross-page purpose

The homepage introduces both studies in one short paragraph, then gives a single research spotlight whose 5/2/2/1 case comparison is concrete and accessible. The CV uses field-appropriate bibliographic descriptions and compact methodological bullets. The Research overview presents the two projects together and adds an expressly bounded **Luis/S20** analogy, moving to uninvestigated questions about student influence over classroom interpretation. The scoping detail page explains the reporting and screening logic, and the action page reports the detailed assessments and evidence limitations. These different editorial purposes justify repeating identifying facts and a representative example. The text does not repeat an identical generic motivation paragraph across every page.

The sample prose reviewed has varied actors and transitions, avoids unsupported causal results and has a restrained, attentive voice. No em dash or mechanical three-part rhetoric is introduced. No published wording revision is warranted simply to make the pages sound less related. For a prospective PhD reviewer, the scoping preprint and unpublished (under-review, author-reported) action manuscript are distinguishable, with explicit source and interpretation limitations.
