## Source-to-claim coverage

Reviewed the existing page sections on scientific sex development, classroom epistemic agency and cultural recognition, with the neighbouring paragraphs, citations and catalogue entries. Direct external sources consulted in this stage:

- **Arboleda, Sandberg and Vilain (2014)**, *DSDs: Genetics, underlying pathologies and psychosexual differentiation*, [Nature Reviews Endocrinology publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrendo.2014.130) and [full NIH article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4441533/). Review separates gonadal determination from later hormonal/tissue differentiation and documents variation in regulatory pathways. The website accurately distinguishes these stages and limits the clinical categories' applicability to teaching.
- **Witchel (2018)**, *Disorders of sex development*, [Elsevier publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1521693417301955) and [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29503125/). Reviews coordinated chromosomal, gonadal and anatomical development. Source supports the biological variation statement without determining a student's body or gender from karyotype.
- **Asghar (2013)**, *Canadian and Pakistani Muslim teachers’ perceptions of evolutionary science and evolution education*, [Springer full article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1936-6434-6-10). Interview and focus-group study of **25 high-school teachers** examines religious commitments, classroom evolution instruction and gaps in evolutionary mechanism knowledge. Research page neither generalizes findings to all Muslim teachers nor accepts misconceptions as scientific alternatives.
- **Alsop and Watts (2000)**, *Facts and feelings*, [IOP original article](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/35/2/311). Comparative study of two groups of physics learners and their feelings around radioactivity. The website's contextual summary matches publisher's abstract; full article text not retrieved.
- **Gonsalves (2014)**, *“Physics and the girly girl—there is a contradiction somewhere”: Doctoral students’ positioning around discourses of gender and competence in physics*, [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11422-012-9447-6) and cross-citation publication record. Reports positioning and recognition among physics doctoral students. The existing bibliographic em dash is part of the published title and must not be altered to enforce a prose-only style preference; original text beyond available abstract was not all checked.
- **Miller et al. (2018)**, *Addressing the epistemic elephant in the room*, [Wiley publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/tea.21459). The critique of preselected practices and canonical knowledge supports the website's statement that epistemic agency is more than performing prescribed practices.
- **Krist et al. (2023)**, *Teacher Noticing for Supporting Students’ Epistemic Agency in Science Sensemaking Discussions*, [Stanford research record](https://connectscied.stanford.edu/publications/teaching-epistemic-agency/teacher-noticing-supporting-students-epistemic-agency) and [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1405055). Teacher responses and the decisions that open or close opportunities for collective student knowledge-building are supported.

## Findings and editorial decision

The Research overview's published theoretical and empirical descriptions have appropriate context and do not treat clinical categories, religious commitments or interdisciplinary interview results as interchangeable with secondary biology classroom evidence. Its prospective pedagogical example is explicitly hypothetical. The passages connecting identity, recognition and science authority use meaningful transitions without turning the cited scholarship into a claim that the author's untested interventions succeeded.
