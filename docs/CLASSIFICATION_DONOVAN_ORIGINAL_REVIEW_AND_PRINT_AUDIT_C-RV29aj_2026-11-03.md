## Authentic original article and access level

Brian M. Donovan, “Ending genetic essentialism through genetics education,” *Human Genetics and Genomics Advances* **3**(1), **100058**, issue dated **13 January 2022**, DOI **10.1016/j.xhgg.2021.100058**, with earlier electronic publication in September 2021. This is **an open-access authored literature review**, not one newly conducted randomized trial and not evidence from the website author's own classroom. Verified original records include [the publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666247721000397), [PubMed bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35047848/), and [the original full article text at PubMed Central PMCID PMC8756492](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8756492/).

The original full-body article **was read as HTML**, including its paragraphs on racialized prevalence in genetics textbooks, the evidence for psychological genetic essentialism, its distinct review of basic genetics, standard genomics and purposefully anti-essentialist genomics instruction, the summarized randomized studies and the paper's account of future research gaps. **No PDF file was analyzed or page screenshot claimed.** The original article is substantively available; the 29 October source-metadata review was more limited and its historical audit should not be rewritten retroactively.

**Research evidence most relevant to the essay:** The author summarizes **three randomized controlled studies** with middle/high school students in which learning about prevalence of single-gene disorders by racialized groups produced stronger measured essentialist racial beliefs than comparison instruction. One explicitly described earlier trial compared **43 eighth-grade students**, some learning about racial differences in disorders including sickle-cell anaemia and others learning prevalence without racial categories. The review reports higher endorsement of inferred racial genetic differences in academic and intellectual potential among students exposed to racialized materials. Two further randomized studies in **Grades 7–9** replicated the broader pattern. These were studies carried out by Donovan and colleagues and reviewed in the later 2022 article; the essay cites the **review**, not each independently verified original trial. The review also synthesizes **other** studies of instructional approaches constructed to challenge essentialism. It reports positive changes under specified instructional conditions without proving all race discussions, all genetics lessons or all countries produce the same effect.

The findings are **about changes in measured student beliefs under specified teaching conditions**, not evidence that race can predict a given individual's HbS genotype or that the website author's hypothetical lesson improves attitudes. Its causal support derives from the reviewed randomized comparisons, while application to the site's proposed classroom investigations remains an educational interpretation. The author does not claim a new curriculum intervention of their own.

The sentence moves from empirical comparison to interpretation with a natural concept bridge rather than a formulaic transition.
