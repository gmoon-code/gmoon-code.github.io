## Three original sources, distinct claim roles

**Schwarz, Reiser, Davis et al. (2009).** [Original *Journal of Research in Science Teaching* 46(6), 632–654](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.20311). The original publisher's free-access article abstract and ERIC original indexed record describe a learning progression built on two dimensions, models used as tools **for predicting and explaining**, and models **changing as learners' understanding improves**. The paper includes illustration from Grades 5–6 and foregrounds knowledge about what scientific models do, not merely the addition of detail to a picture. The website's concise two-dimensional formulation is **accurate**. Verification tier **A**, original journal article abstract and original indexed abstract, not a new original full-text line-by-line check.

**Berland, Schwarz, Krist et al. (2016).** [Original journal bibliographic record](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.21257) and the [research team/publication description](https://connectscied.stanford.edu/publications/foundational/epistemologies-practice-making-scientific-practices-meaningful-students). The authors develop an **Epistemologies in Practice (EIP)** conceptual framework that attends to students' epistemic goals for knowledge construction and their understandings of how to undertake that work. The website's sentence about the scientific questions students are trying to resolve shaping the significance of classroom practices is a faithful conceptual paraphrase. **Tier A**, original research-team abstract and original publication information, not a fully verified study-specific intervention effect.

**Manz (2015).** [Original *Review of Educational Research* publisher article and abstract](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314558490), 85(4), 553–590, with an [ERIC original indexed record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1081789). The author identifies differences between scientific and school settings for **argumentation**, and suggests situating it within genuinely uncertain scientific activity and attending to students' epistemic cultures and resources. This is a literature and conceptual analysis, **not** a new controlled test of a teacher's particular model-critique intervention. The website's linked sentence, "The questions students are trying to resolve also shape how scientific practices and argumentation become meaningful", is **accurate when read with Berland and Manz together**. **Tier A**, original publisher abstract plus ERIC article record.

## Interpreting the continuous paragraph

The preceding comparison between **Murphy et al. (2023)** and **Cheung & Yang (2026)**, newly checked in C-RV24c, illustrates an important difference between **students revising evaluative criteria** and **students applying criteria previously selected by adults**. Schwarz supplies the specific conceptual trajectory that models serve explanatory and predictive purposes and can change through critique. Berland and Manz then explain why students' questions and epistemic purposes affect the meaning of engaging in modelling and argumentation. This is a coherent, restrained argument; no need to replace it with a forced conclusion that participation necessarily transfers authority or improves conceptual learning.

All three journal citation years, article titles, DOIs and the student's grade-level/genre implications match their original indexed records. The exact meaning is supported, and the site neither implies all three are empirical interventions nor generalizes the illustrations into a universal learning gain.
