## Source, provenance, inspection and limitations

**Carlone, Heidi B., and Angela Johnson (2007), "Understanding the science experiences of successful women of color: Science identity as an analytic lens."** Original [Wiley publisher citation/abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/tea.20237) identifies *Journal of Research in Science Teaching*, 44(8), 1187–1218; first online 11 September 2007; DOI `10.1002/tea.20237`. The original article was retrieved in a [University of North Carolina at Greensboro institutional repository PDF](https://libres.uncg.edu/ir/uncg/f/H_Carlone_Understanding_2007.pdf). Its first page was successfully **visually screenshot-inspected using the PDF source tool**, confirming the title, authors, journal identity and acknowledgement that the repository version may omit some figures. The PDF source's text was directly read for the abstract, primary interview design, development of the science-identity framework, analysis of recognition, competing trajectories, interpretation and explicit limitations. The publisher record corroborates bibliographic information; source-level methodological claims derive from original article passages. Because the institutional PDF warns that some figures may be missing, **no assertion of complete source figure verification** is made.

The study draws on **15 women of colour**, their undergraduate science experiences, interviews approximately **six years later** and member-checking. It develops the related analytic concepts of competence, performance and recognition, and interprets research-scientist, altruistic-scientist and disrupted-scientist trajectories. Recognition within science departments was especially important for some trajectories, while other participants created alternative ways of identifying with science and valued recognition from community members or the people their scientific work could benefit. The disruptions were interpreted within the women's reported experiences of race, ethnicity and gender.

**Methodological qualification:** The article explicitly states that the researchers mainly relied on the women's accounts of how they were recognized, and that they **did not directly observe** many of the reported encounters with scientific others. Thus it cannot independently determine the intentions of the professors or other people described in those stories. This evidence tier confirms the authors' analytic interpretation and participant-reported experiences, not an observational finding about a teacher's hidden motives or a causal estimate of identity outcomes.

## Public paragraph and writing assessment

Current `research.html#cultural-knowledge` states:

> A longitudinal study followed fifteen women of colour from undergraduate science studies into later careers, finding that recognition by others mattered differently across their science-identity trajectories (Carlone & Johnson, 2007).

The numerical sample, educational trajectory and differentiation of recognition are directly supported by the primary article. The source's contrast between recognition by science professionals, recognition from communities and recognition withheld or disrupted confirms the precise phrase **"mattered differently"**. The website makes no claim that every woman faced the same experience, or that a researcher directly observed each exchange. Its sentence follows the separate Gonsalves doctoral-physics case and precedes a qualified author reflection about judgments by which someone is recognized as a participant in science. These are related but distinct educational settings. The existing transitions are natural and cohesive, with no unsupported move from university science-identity research to measured outcomes in the author's own secondary school.

The adjacent community science club reference by Burke and Navas Iannini (2021) was checked against the [original Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/tea.21714), which identifies 202 surveyed club participants, 45 focus-group contributors and its findings about hands-on club science, relationships and difficulty connecting club enjoyment with school science. The website's corresponding description remains appropriate. This **abstract-only** Burke check does **not** qualify as a new F-tier full-text read.

**Public editorial disposition:** No public HTML change was warranted.
