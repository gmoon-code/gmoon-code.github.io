## Exact public text and content warrants

The current `research.html#curriculum-decisions` states:

> Curricular planning can make room for students to influence the methods of investigation and the models through which a class develops an explanation (Ko & Krist, 2019).

Ko and Krist identify three potentially opened curricular dimensions: investigation methods, students' developing explanatory models, and the phenomena/questions anchoring instruction. They interpret video-based case material from **Grade 6 and Grade 8** classrooms, selecting three paradigmatic episodes from five candidate lesson sets to show different ways teachers initiated or planned openings for student judgment. Their **conceptual reach** is an analytic framework, not a demonstrated causal gain or proof that opening every level is always desirable. The authors explicitly caution that curricular openness without taking student contributions seriously is insufficient to redistribute epistemic agency, and distinguish theory-driven reach from longer-term empirically tracked development. The present public sentence claims a *possibility* grounded in curriculum planning, and the next two sentences explicitly retain teachers' interpretive authority during enactment. Its qualifications are appropriate.

The current `research.html#cultural-knowledge` states:

> Recognition also concerns who is considered capable of scientific work. Doctoral physics students described how expectations about gender and competence affected the ways they could position themselves as physicists (Gonsalves, 2014).

Gonsalves's original article directly supports that statement. Recognition in physics was negotiated through gendered expectations and several conceptions of competence. The study treats gender positions as socially and locally produced, attending to participants who resisted or reworked conventional expectations. Its sample concerns **doctoral physics** at a particular department; the website does **not** claim secondary students or the website author's own classes were studied. The neighbouring reference to the different longitudinal science-identity study of fifteen women of colour remains a separate population and analytical purpose, with a qualified final reflection on judgments of recognition rather than a manufactured common empirical outcome.
