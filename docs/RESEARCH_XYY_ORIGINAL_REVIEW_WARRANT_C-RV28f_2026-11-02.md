## Public statement and exact source-level interpretation

The current `research.html#sex-classification` paragraph states:

> Historical research demonstrates why the uses of a category deserve scrutiny alongside its biological basis. Earlier attempts to link an XYY chromosome pattern with aggression drew on cultural assumptions about masculinity, an episode examined in the history of sex-chromosome research (Richardson, 2013). A later systematic review found no statistical evidence that an extra Y chromosome, by itself, establishes a predisposition to aggression or criminal behaviour (Re & Birkhoff, 2015). The earlier inference illustrates how a chromosome finding was extended into a claim about behaviour without adequate evidence.

**Warrant:** The original review concludes that the evidence does not establish that 47,XYY by genotype **alone** confers the proposed aggression/deviance predisposition. It also discusses studies in prison/institutional populations with severe sample-selection limitations. In its discussion, the authors differentiate selected prisoner samples from longitudinal or other evidence and refer to a 2012 study in which apparently raised overall conviction risk was no longer different after accounting for socioeconomic factors. The review's own final paragraph cautiously describes a possible multi-factor contribution while **rejecting a direct, sole genetic explanation**. The website's particular restriction **“by itself”** is therefore important. It is not an assertion that chromosome complements can never correlate with any clinical or behavioural phenotype or that all observational comparisons are null.

The historical-culture claim in the preceding sentence is separately attributed to Richardson (2013), whose original book/chapter metadata and abstract were documented at A tier in C-RV24e. This review is not being substituted for the evidence of the cultural-history interpretation. In the next paragraph, the website's discussion of medical definitions and hormone-based assumptions concerns distinct forms of inferred authority. The movement from historical XYY explanations to institutional use of medical categories is conceptually coherent, with sufficient distinction between a genotype measurement and a claim about an individual's social conduct.

**Editorial decision:** No public HTML edit is warranted. The existing sentence avoids statistical universals and keeps the author's natural, restrained Canadian-English wording. Expanding it to discuss every included criminal-justice cohort or the authors' tentative possible risk-factor framing would add more detail than the website's illustrative scholarly-interest purpose requires. The statement is source-faithful and contributes to the paragraph's progression.
