Ye Eun Moon

I teach biology and chemistry to secondary students in South Korea. Some of the questions that stay with me begin with an explanation a student has written or a question that changes the direction of a discussion. I try to understand what students are thinking before deciding how to respond. Those experiences have gradually shaped my research and the learning resources I make.

Research

I'm interested in what a student's response allows a teacher to understand and what it may leave uncertain.

Preregistered scoping review · 2026

Evidence Routes and Validity Risk in Claims About Multilingual Learners' Science Understanding in Secondary Science

I reviewed how secondary science studies use writing, classroom interaction, and visual representations to make claims about multilingual learners' understanding. The work considers how task demands and researchers' judgments affect what the evidence can support.

Classroom action research · 2026

Relationships Among Post-Assessment Reflection, Reconstruction, and Later Mechanistic Reasoning in Advanced Placement Biology

In one AP Biology class, I followed 28 students' explanations across three assessments and considered them alongside reflections, preparation records, and causal reconstructions. I wanted to understand what changed in their explanations and what each record could tell me.

A story-based AP Biology learning environment. Students can move through connected scenes, revisit scientific representations, and work through review and challenge activities. A separate Content Studio helps me organize and revise the curriculum.

Biology · Chemistry · Life science · Student inquiry

Making room for students to explain their thinking

I use investigations, diagrams, models, reading, and discussion to help students work through scientific ideas. When an explanation is incomplete, I try to look at the relationships a student has made visible before deciding what support might help. I am still learning how to do this well in a multilingual classroom.
