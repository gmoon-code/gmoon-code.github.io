Science teaching, research, and things I make

Ye Eun Moon

Hello, I'm Ye Eun. I teach biology and chemistry in South Korea, where students' questions often draw my attention to relationships I had assumed were clear. I enjoy following those questions with them, whether we are tracing a biological process or trying to understand what a set of measurements can tell us.

This website gathers some of the teaching materials, research questions, and learning tools that have grown from that work. I have also made room for the music and stories I enjoy outside school. Thank you for visiting.

Questions I am investigating

Research

My research includes a scoping review of how studies interpret multilingual students' scientific work and a classroom action research inquiry into post-assessment reflection. These inquiries have led me to ask whose questions influence classroom investigations and how teachers respond when students challenge an explanation or classification.

Learning tools to explore

Student research · A guided workspace

Research Methods Studio

A research question often changes once a student begins examining the sources, measurements, or methods available. I built this workspace to help students return to earlier decisions as they develop a study, from the first question through design, analysis, and writing.

Try the workspace ↗ Explore the design →

90 seeds, 18 experimental units

In a synthetic radish experiment, salinity treatments are assigned to Petri dishes, each containing five seeds. The dataset has ninety seed records from eighteen independently assigned dishes. That distinction affects how the observations can be analysed.

I have also built The Story Method, an AP Biology learning environment in which fictional journeys lead into recall and application activities.
