Ye Eun Moon

Hello, I'm Ye Eun Moon, a science educator in South Korea preparing for doctoral study. I am interested in how science curricula influence what students are invited to investigate and whose knowledge can contribute to that work. When a student questions a familiar model or suggests examining different evidence, what happens next depends partly on how teachers and classmates respond. I hope to study those decisions and explore how students and teachers might develop scientific inquiries together.

My questions about participation are inseparable from concerns about racism, colonial histories, and inequalities involving gender, sexuality, class, and language. These conditions can influence whose knowledge is recognised and which experiences and questions find room in science education. I hope to contribute to anti-racist, anti-colonial, and queer- and trans-inclusive curricula, with biological sex and human variation offering one area for further inquiry. My scoping review of multilingual science education and classroom action research in AP Biology have given me experience examining how scientific reasoning is expressed and interpreted. I have gathered those studies, curriculum work, and learning tools here, alongside some of my interests beyond school.

Research and teaching

Completed inquiries and developing questions

Research

My scoping review examines how 20 studies interpreted multilingual students' scientific work. In a separate classroom action research inquiry, I followed 28 AP Biology students' explanations across assessments, feedback, and reconstruction. Both inquiries have made me more careful about the conclusions I draw from a single response, a concern that informs the broader questions about participation and curriculum I hope to pursue.

Learning tools to explore

Student research · A guided workspace

Research Methods Studio

A research question often changes once a student begins examining the sources, measurements, or methods available. I built this workspace to help students return to earlier decisions as they develop a study, from the first question through design, analysis, and writing.

Try the workspace ↗ Explore the design →

90 seeds, 18 experimental units

In a synthetic radish experiment, salinity treatments are assigned to eighteen Petri dishes, each containing five seeds. Although the dataset records ninety seeds, it is the dishes that receive independent treatment assignments. Students must account for that distinction when deciding what constitutes a replicate in their analysis.

I have also built The Story Method, an AP Biology learning environment in which fictional journeys lead into recall and application activities.
