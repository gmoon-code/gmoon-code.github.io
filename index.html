Justice-oriented science education · Research and teaching

Ye Eun Moon

I teach biology and chemistry in South Korea and am preparing for doctoral study in science education. My interests concern how scientific knowledge, classroom authority, and the histories carried into science curricula influence which questions students can pursue and whose contributions shape an investigation. My research on multilingual science education and AP Biology explanations has led me to ask how students and teachers might develop more just ways of investigating the world together.

From the research records

Classroom action research · AP Biology · 28 students

What does an explanation after correction establish?

In my classroom inquiry, ten students independently reconstructed a photosynthesis mechanism after correcting an assessment response. Some repaired missing causal connections, while others omitted relationships present in their earlier explanations. Comparing the paired responses changed the questions I needed to ask about their reasoning.

One comparison from the study

S20's original explanation included relationships involving the proton gradient, ATP, and NADPH. Those relationships were absent from the later reconstruction, raising questions about what the student could explain under each task's conditions.
