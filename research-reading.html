Research reading · Selected publications

Questions arising from scholarly reading

My research interests continue to develop through reading across science education, curriculum studies, multilingual education and participatory research. These selected first-authored works help me pose more specific questions about classroom evidence, authority and the opportunities students have to influence an inquiry.

The brief annotations identify connections I am examining. They are reading notes based on the published works and available summaries, rather than a comprehensive review or claims that these authors share a single approach.

Participation, inquiry and classroom authority

These works raise questions about how students and teachers establish the direction of an investigation, and how authority operates in formal and informal learning settings.

  1. Rishi Krishnamoorthy, Ravit Golan Duncan and Edna Tan (2026). That’s Just Gonna Make Them Upset: Youth Authoring Emerging Epistemic Ideals Through Rightful Presence.

    The community-survey episode invites attention to how students' concern for respondents changed the criteria used to design evidence collection.

  2. Lydia E. Carol-Ann Burke and Ana Maria Navas Iannini (2021). Science Engagement as Insight into the Science Identity Work Nurtured in Community-Based Science Clubs.

    Students' accounts of school and club science raise a question about whether recognition in an informal setting carries into school science.

  3. James D. Slotta and Marcia C. Linn (2009). WISE Science: Web-Based Inquiry in the Classroom.

    The account of inquiry curriculum design provides a point of comparison for how learning environments organise evidence, guidance and opportunities for revision.

  4. Anton Puvirajah and Brendan Calandra (2015). Embodied Experiences in Virtual Worlds Role-Play as a Conduit for Novice Teacher Identity Exploration.

    The case of novice teacher role-play raises questions about how a learning setting makes particular professional identities and forms of participation available.

  5. Richard Reeve (2023). Meeting Students Halfway: Materials in an Ideas-Centered Classroom.

    The Grade 6 design case shows why a teacher's intended inquiry sequence may need to change as students work with models and materials.

  6. Sarah Elizabeth Barrett (2013). Becoming an Activist Science Teacher: A Longitudinal Case Study.

    A longitudinal account of a teacher's commitments offers a way to ask how intentions, school conditions and enacted decisions relate over time.

Knowledge, difference and curricular responsibility

Here the common concern is how scientific or educational categories acquire authority, and what responsibility educators have when those categories affect students' lives.

  1. Allison J. Gonsalves (2014). “Physics and the Girly Girl—There Is a Contradiction Somewhere”: Doctoral Students’ Positioning around Discourses of Gender and Competence in Physics.

    The study examines how gendered accounts of competence enter disciplinary identity work, offering questions for how scientific belonging is negotiated.

  2. Anila Asghar (2013). Canadian and Pakistani Muslim Teachers’ Perceptions of Evolutionary Science and Evolution Education.

    The cross-context interviews make it important to distinguish the biology being explained from assumptions about learners' and teachers' beliefs.

  3. Isha DeCoito (2024). STEM Education: Curriculum and Pedagogy.

    The chapter connects student-directed STEM inquiry with teachers' planning and disciplinary guidance, a practical tension in my own curriculum work.

  4. Steve Alsop and Mike Watts (2000). Facts and Feelings: Exploring the Affective Domain in the Learning of Physics.

    Comparing students' responses to an emotive science topic raises questions about what affect contributes to how learners approach disciplinary explanations.

  5. Jen Gilbert (2021). Getting Dirty and Coming Clean: Sex Education and the Problem of Expertise.

    The account of expertise and sex education provides a way to scrutinise the relation between curricular decisions, institutional authority and students' rights.

  6. Qui Dorian Alexander (2023). TERF Logics Are Carceral Logics: Toward the Abolition of Gender-Critical Movements or Black Trans Life as Pedagogical Praxis.

    This commentary presses questions about how educational knowledge and institutional categories can enact exclusion, and whose knowledge can challenge them.

  7. Lee Airton (2023). You Don’t Know Me: Welcoming Gender Diversity in Schools via an Ethic of Hospitality.

    The discussion of school policy invites care about whom institutional responses make legible and whether a student is expected to disclose personal information.

  8. Casey Burkholder, JJ Wright and Melissa Keehn (2025). Queer Joy as Pedagogy.

    Participatory arts projects frame joy as a collective pedagogical practice and raise methodological questions about how participants shape the research.

Multilingual meaning-making and assessment

These publications help distinguish the resources learners use from the evidence a particular task makes visible, while keeping institutional and social conditions in view.

  1. Shakina Rajendram (2023). Translanguaging as an Agentive Pedagogy for Multilingual Learners: Affordances and Constraints.

    The distinction between learner initiative and classroom constraints is pertinent when tracing what happens to a question first expressed in another language.

  2. Angelica Galante (2022). Affordances of Plurilingual Instruction in Higher Education: A Mixed Methods Study with a Quasi-Experiment in an English Language Program.

    Comparing instructional conditions makes it important to specify which outcomes the study measured and how learners experienced the language options offered.

  3. Gail Prasad (2015). Beyond the Mirror Towards a Plurilingual Prism: Exploring the Creation of Plurilingual Identity Texts in English and French Classrooms in Toronto and Montpellier.

    Students' identity-text production offers a point of comparison for how representations and research conversations reveal dimensions of meaning absent from a final text alone.

  4. Jennifer Burton (2024). Critical Pedagogy and Antiracist Practices in Language Teaching with Spoken Word Poetry.

    Two classroom situations foreground the tensions and emotions involved in antiracist teaching, rather than presenting an inclusive activity as sufficient by itself.

  5. Heike Neumann, Stephanie Kozak and Leslie Gil (2023). The Impact of Academic Acculturation and Language Proficiency on International Students’ University Experience and Academic Success: A Longitudinal Study.

    The study cautions against treating a language measure as a self-contained account of academic performance when other conditions also shape outcomes.

  6. Rob Simon (2011). On the Human Challenges of Multiliteracies Pedagogy.

    The argument for relational and culturally situated literacy teaching invites closer examination of teachers' assumptions about what children can contribute.

The works listed here differ in setting, methods and purpose. Their findings cannot simply be transferred to my AP Biology classroom. As I read the full articles and develop a doctoral research design, I will need to examine their evidence and limits more closely, including places where their commitments or assumptions differ from mine.
